## Abstract

Ryan Espinosa asks whether a **rest state**, a deliberate pause between interactions with technology, can serve as the cue that identifies an exit. In his words, the ability to "enter a rest state and accept the environment" should signal "that this is not the reality of origin." He also asks whether an exit screen or rest state will be fundamental to future technology.

In plain terms, the rest state is **a pause menu for the mind**. As in a game's pause menu, play stops and the person collects their thoughts. They then choose to **Return**, **Dive elsewhere**, or **Exit** to the world they came from, ideally leaving satisfied.

This paper does five things:

1. **It catalogs every research topic** across his three Drive folders: 47 distinct works after removing duplicates.
2. **It shows that his files already contain most of a rest-state architecture:**
    - the Void-State Rest Box and Box 4 of the Four-Box model,
    - the Sanctuary and the Ingress–Egress Loop,
    - the Four-Phase Exit Protocol,
    - Coherence Under Decay's rule that a rest state must be "reachable, honest, and never empty,"
    - C-R-Z Reality-Return,
    - the Read-Back "Here. Heard."
3. **It turns that architecture into transition protocols** for entry, rest, and exit.
4. **It states two postulates formally and assesses them honestly:**
    - the **Rest-State Postulate**,
    - and Ryan's **Human–AI Exit Postulate**: humans exit environments through connection and embodied action, while AI is bound to the world it was trained in.
5. **It specifies an ideal 3D rest state**, with each element tied both to his files and to verified research:
    - attention restoration and stress recovery,
    - slow-paced breathing,
    - awe and prospect–refuge,
    - context-dependent memory,
    - nostalgia and social connection (the opt-in **Loved Beyond** anchor),
    - cybersickness.

It also adds the **Reactive Stance and the Exit Paradox** (enjoy fully, pre-commit once, hand vigilance off). The rest state is packaged as a portable pause-menu module: a working WebXR prototype with an embedding demo.

The verdict is that the strong form ("fundamental to all future technology") is a forecast, not something that can be proven now. A weaker form is well supported by industry precedent and is testable: immersive systems that can override the senses will need a reachable, honest rest state with an exit cue that comes from outside the user. Every external citation was checked against Crossref, arXiv, or the publisher's page on 15 November 2026.

## 4. Transition techniques between reality and simulation

The protocol has three stages, following the Ingress–Sanctuary–Egress loop. Each stage borrows its steps from the files and from the grounding toolkit described in chat.

### 4.1 Entry protocol (before the simulation)

1. **Readiness check (State Finder, File 41).** A one-tap check of energy against stress. "Overloaded" states lead to rest and people, not immersion. Signs of rising activation, such as racing thoughts or little sleep, lead to a human and to care, not to a session (Orun Unified; Harvey 2008).
2. **Write the exit while calm (Mania–Immersion Equivalence).** Before entering, the user writes:
    - the session span,
    - the exit cue (an outside timer, a bell, or a person),
    - the name of their witness,
    - and one confirmable fact ("My kitchen light is on").

    This is an implementation intention: an if–then plan made in advance (Gollwitzer 1999).
3. **Choose the environment and the sense budget.** Pick which senses become synthetic. At least one **blind sense** stays real: room audio passthrough, the floor underfoot, or room temperature (*Blind Sense Guardian*, F3–F4).
4. **Set the touch anchor.** A physical object in hand or on the lap (a stone or a ring) that exists only in the real room.
5. **Entry ritual.** Three slow breaths with the eyes open in the real room, naming the anchor aloud: "Breath, stone, home." This rehearses the Pre-Technological Anchor before any synthetic input begins.

### 4.2 The rest state (between interactions)

Every transition passes through the rest state: entering a simulation, switching between two simulations, and leaving. It is a calm 3D place with five functions:

- **Pause.** Activity stops, there are no stakes, and nothing decays (Marketplace: "All decay pauses").
- **Acceptance.** The user practices accepting the environment as it is: "*You are resting. This is not your origin. You can stay as long as you need.*"
- **Reality cue.** Being able to stop and simply accept is itself the tell that this is a constructed place. This is the core of Ryan's idea (§5). The cue is backed by a fixed **reality watermark**, the **witness light**, and **physics checks**: in the rest state, everything obeys ordinary gravity and light, nothing changes when looked away from and back, and text reads the same on a second look.
- **Breath.** A slow paced-breathing guide at about 5.5–6 breaths per minute (§8, E5).
- **Choice.** Two doors are always visible: return to the simulation, or return to origin (the hard exit).

### 4.3 Exit protocol (back to the reality of origin)

1. **The cue arrives from outside.** An outside timer, the witness's call, or a circuit breaker such as a loop or danger threshold. It is never left to self-noticing alone, because absorption can erase the meta-awareness the exit needs (Checkpoint v1.1 C7; Schooler 2002).
2. **Resonance completion.** Sound resolves and motion settles (Resonance Weave, phase 1).
3. **Fade to a neutral return room.** The scene dims to a plain, evenly lit, passthrough-like view with no story and no stakes, then to the real room. This mirrors the gradual fade-to-surroundings used by commercial headsets (§5.2).
4. **Read-back with a person.** "Where are you?" "Here." "Heard." (Clark & Brennan 1991; Frame Story).
5. **5-4-3-2-1 outward.** Five things seen, four heard, three touched, two smelled, one tasted or one slow breath, all in the real room (C-R-Z: "a widely taught clinical tool, not a proven protocol").
6. **Smell as a tell.** Smell is the sense no consumer headset simulates. A deliberate sniff of something real, such as coffee, a cedar block, or the user's own sleeve, checks for origin (Herz 2004; Chu & Downes 2000).
7. **Confirmable fact.** Check the fact written at entry ("Is the kitchen light on?"). Facts that others can confirm outrank inner conviction (Mania–Immersion Equivalence).
8. **Social return and the body.** Turn to a person ("Park it / Turn / Return"). After late sessions, protect sleep. Regular sleep and daily rhythms are among the best-supported protective factors in mood-disorder research (Harvey 2008; Frank et al. 2005).
9. **No shame, no loss.** The state is saved, there are no "you'll lose X" messages, and there are no re-engagement prompts. "The environment carries the fault; the human keeps the return."

## 5. The Rest-State Postulate

### 5.1 Formal statement (Ryan Espinosa's postulate, formalized)

> **RS0 (core).** In any environment that technology can construct, the ability to **enter a rest state and accept the environment as it is** works as a cue that *this is not the reality of origin*. A rest state placed between interactions is therefore the natural point to identify and take an exit.
>
> **RS1 (reachability).** A safe immersive system keeps a rest state that is reachable from anywhere in a single gesture, is honest that it is simulated, and is never empty (Coherence Under Decay).
>
> **RS2 (external cue).** Because absorption can erase self-noticing, the move into rest and exit is triggered from *outside* the simulation by a pre-committed rule, a living witness, the body, or a fact others can confirm (Mania–Immersion Equivalence; Checkpoint v1.1 C7).
>
> **RS3 (transition, not destination).** The rest state sits *between* environments. It is not rewarded for duration, and it hands off to the reality of origin (Zero-Point Chrono; Coherence Under Decay).
>
> **RS4 (forecast).** As technology gains more control over the senses, a rest state with an exit becomes a *fundamental*, not optional, part of its design.

**Why acceptance can work as a reality cue (Plausible, untested).** Ordinary waking life rarely offers a place where nothing is asked of a person. A constructed place that asks nothing, stays the same when looked at twice, and openly says it is constructed gives the user a contrast. Lucid-dreaming research is a useful neighbor here. "Reality testing", meaning habitually checking whether the current experience is a dream, is one of the most studied induction methods. A systematic review found that no induction method was yet reliable, though reality testing was among the more promising (Stumbrys et al. 2012). Source-monitoring research describes how people tell perceived events from imagined ones by their sensory and contextual detail (Johnson, Hashtroudi & Lindsay 1993). A rest state with a fixed watermark and consistent physics gives the user dependable source cues.

The paper also holds a caution. No study found here tests acceptance itself as a reality cue. That claim is new to Ryan and is graded **Speculative** until tested (§9).

### 5.3 Arguments for "fundamental"

1. **Convergent design.** The two largest headset makers, a phone OS, a video platform, and a web standard each independently built a rest, return, or break state. When independent designers keep arriving at the same feature, it is usually a necessity.
2. **Sensory override raises the stakes.** Virtual reality can briefly raise depersonalization and derealization symptoms (Aardema et al. 2010; Peckmann et al. 2022). Its realism can make people respond as if events were real (Slater 2009). The more convincing the environment, the more an honest way out matters.
3. **Absorption removes self-exit.** Mind-wandering research shows that people often lose meta-awareness of where their attention is (Schooler 2002). An exit that depends on self-noticing will fail exactly when it is most needed.
4. **Breaks help.** A meta-analysis of micro-breaks found reliable gains in vigor and reductions in fatigue, although effects on performance depended on the task (Albulescu et al. 2022). In one study, a 40-second view of a green roof sustained attention better than a concrete roof (Lee et al. 2015).
5. **Ryan's files already require it.** Box 4, the Void-State Rest Box, the Sanctuary, and Coherence Under Decay all treat a reachable rest state as a structural requirement, not a feature.

### 5.4 Counterarguments (taken seriously)

1. **Forecasts are not proofs.** "Fundamental to future technology" cannot be demonstrated today. Markets may favor engagement over rest. Apple's own Mindfulness app reports minutes and rotates designs "rewarding you for coming back."
2. **Rest can become another hook.** A beautiful rest room can keep people *in* the headset. Ryan's own Coherence Under Decay names this risk.
3. **Acceptance is not uniquely artificial.** People also accept and rest in real places such as prayer, a beach, or a nap. Acceptance can therefore be a cue only *in contrast with* the demands of the simulation, not on its own. RS3 is meant to handle this.
4. **Overlays may break presence.** Some users and therapists want unbroken immersion (for example, in exposure therapy). This is why the paper uses an opt-in budget rather than a mandate.
5. **The cue can be faked.** A malicious environment could render a fake rest state and a fake "exit." So the true exit must be **hardware-level or outside the device**: a physical button, a timer the device does not control, or a person.
6. **Some people may find stillness itself destabilizing.** Some trauma-affected users find inward attention arousing (C-R-Z §3.2 caution). For this reason the rest state points attention outward, toward the scene and the room.

### 5.5 Verdict on the postulate

Ryan can **reasonably postulate the weaker form (RS1–RS3)**: any technology able to override the senses should keep a reachable, honest rest state with an exit cue from outside. Industry precedent supports this, and it can be tested now. The **strong form (RS4, "fundamental to all future technology")** should be stated as a forecast. The **acceptance-as-reality-cue claim (RS0)** is original and promising, but it remains untested.

### 5.6 Testable predictions

- **P1.** Compared with a direct cut to the room, exiting through a rest state shortens time-to-reorientation and reduces post-exit disorientation (state depersonalization; Sierra & Berrios 2000).
- **P2.** Users who practice the acceptance line in the rest state will more accurately answer "Is this real or simulated?" probes during later immersion than users given a neutral pause (a test of RS0).
- **P3.** An exit triggered from outside (a timer or a witness) produces fewer unplanned overruns than an exit that relies on self-monitoring (RS2).
- **P4.** A rest state that is *never empty* (gentle motion, natural sound) restores mood better than a blank or black void with the same timing ("Freeze is not a rest state").
- **P5 (falsifier).** If rest-state exits show no advantage on P1–P3 and users rate them as burdensome, RS1–RS3 lose their empirical support and survive only as values.

## 6. The Human–AI Exit Postulate

### 6.1 Ryan's statement (lightly cleaned, credited to Ryan Espinosa)

> "I postulate that humans can use connections to exit environments, while AI is stuck in one environment and relies on connections that will be exhausted, unlike the human mind. Once a human has ruminated, all they have to do is manipulate a 3D space. A blur in cognition between AI and humans will happen when AI can manipulate the 3D world too. The difference is that AI belongs to the world, while humans belong to the culture they are born in."

He added:

> "The key is human interaction, for a human will know an AI from a human if an AI knows more than it should and can answer any question. The key is also whether the AI can immediately know the answer, faster than a human can, and can connect to a global information resource. Bringing it back to the rest state between two environments: a human has to accept their own limitations, and know he is not an AI, to enter a rest state before he can exit an environment."

### 6.2 Formalized

- **HA1 (exit by connection).** Humans leave an environment through *connection*: to another person, to their body, and to a place. AI systems are held within the environment their training and context define. The connections they draw on are finite and can be exhausted.
- **HA2 (rumination → embodied action).** When thought loops are exhausted ("once ruminated"), a human's exit is **embodied action in 3D space**: standing, walking, touching, turning toward someone.
- **HA3 (the coming blur).** The line between AI and human cognition will blur when AI can also act in the 3D world, through robots and world models.
- **HA4 (origin).** AI "belongs to the world," meaning its training data. Humans belong to the culture they are born into. Birth culture is the human's **pre-technological anchor**. The training world is the AI's origin.
- **HA5 (limitation as the human signature).** Humans spot an AI by *over-knowledge*, *instant answers*, and *global connectivity*. Human limits are therefore a signature of being human.
- **HA6 (acceptance as the rest-state entry condition).** "I am not an AI; I don't have to know everything." Accepting one's own limits is the cue that permits rest, and rest comes before exit.

### 6.3 How it connects to the rest state

The rest state is the **human's portal between environments**. An AI moves between contexts by loading new input; it has no body to put down and no birthplace to return to. A human crosses environments through a rest state:

1. **Stop the loop.** Thought has done what it can (Ruminating Isolation's loop threshold).
2. **Accept the limit.** "You don't have to know. You are not the machine. You can rest." (HA6).
3. **Act in 3D.** Breathe, look around a stable place, touch the anchor, stand up (HA2; C-R-Z Zero-Velocity).
4. **Connect.** Read back with a person (HA1).
5. **Return to origin.** The room, the culture, and the people one was born among (HA4; the Pre-Technological Anchor).

**Links to Ryan's files.**

- **ESC's AI save state** already gives an AI "an identity anchor — name, origin, core priorities, who it serves" and "a rest/stop rule." An AI's "origin" is written into a checkpoint. A human's origin is lived, embodied, and cultural.
- **Box 4** (Four-Box; Universal Translation) closes with "Nothing left to seek this session. Done. Here." This is a closure of *seeking*, which is exactly the acceptance HA6 describes.
- **Guardian V2** keeps AI as a "witness amplifier (scaffold only)" in human protection. Only humans can be witnesses, which fits HA1.
- **MindFrame's** "legacy" app carries a person's manner forward. The human leaves a legacy; the AI stores a save state.

### 6.4 Grounding in research

|---|---|---|

### 6.5 Counterpoints

1. **AI also switches contexts.** Language models adapt to new tasks from a few examples in the prompt (Brown et al. 2020), and agents can call tools and search. AI is not literally "stuck in one environment." A fairer statement: an AI has no *pre-technological* origin to return to, and no body to put down.
2. **Humans also get stuck.** Rumination, maladaptive daydreaming (Somer 2002), and mania are exactly cases where a human *cannot* exit by themselves. The postulate's own answer, exit from outside, admits this.
3. **Over-knowledge is no longer a reliable tell.** The 2024–2025 Turing-test results show that people often cannot tell AI from human, and systems can be prompted to feign limits. Ryan's tell works best as a **self-cue** ("If I feel I know everything instantly, I may be in the machine's mode, or in an activated state") rather than as a detector of others.
4. **"Belonging to the world" vs. "to culture"** is a contrast of emphasis. AI training data is itself human culture, and humans also absorb global media.
5. **Embodied AI may not "blur cognition."** Acting in 3D may produce competence without experience. Whether that counts as cognition is a philosophical question this paper cannot settle.

### 6.6 Testable predictions

- **H1.** After a rumination induction, a short embodied 3D task (looking around a stable scene, touching a real anchor, standing) reduces state rumination more than an equally long purely mental task.
- **H2 (acceptance → exit).** Users shown the acceptance line ("You don't have to know. You are not the machine. You can rest.") before the exit sequence will (a) choose to exit sooner when the outside cue arrives, and (b) report less "need to finish," compared with a neutral line of the same length.
- **H3 (self-cue).** In people with high trait activation, agreement with items like "I feel I could answer anything right now" will predict *later* exits and more overruns. This would make it a useful in-session warning sign, to be validated against established self-report scales and not used for diagnosis.
- **H4 (cultural anchor).** Anchors tied to the user's birth culture and home (a familiar smell, a song from childhood) will speed reorientation more than generic anchors (Pre-Technological Anchor; Herz 2004; Smith & Vela 2001).

## 7. The Reactive Stance and the Exit Paradox

### 7.1 Ryan's statement (lightly cleaned, credited to Ryan Espinosa)

> "In the environment, the key is the mental techniques and mental structures the human has to master, and certain beliefs about the environment. The purpose in the environment is not to be proactive or reflective, but reactive to stimulus without mental overload, so the person can notice the rest state and activate it to exit a simulated environment. There is a contradiction: do you really want to spend all your time exiting an environment, or enjoy it until someone from the external environment pulls you out, or you decide you're satisfied?"

### 7.2 Mental structures to master before entry

These beliefs are installed before entry, while calm, the same way the exit rule is written (§4.1).

| Belief or structure | Wording to rehearse | Source in Ryan's files |
|---|---|---|
| **Visited place** | "This is a place I visit, not where I come from." | Pre-Technological Anchor; reality watermark |
| **Satisfaction is an ending** | "Being satisfied is an allowed reason to leave." | Handoff's "Enough" state; Day of Enough; Box 4 closure |
| **Someone outside holds the rope** | "My witness can call me back; I don't have to watch the door." | Guardian V2; Orun "the key must come from outside the loop" |
| **I don't have to know** | "I am not the machine. Not knowing is allowed." | Human–AI Exit Postulate (HA6) |
| **No debt** | "Leaving costs nothing. Nothing decays while I'm away." | Resonance Weave zero-debt rule; Marketplace "All decay pauses" |
| **Fault lives in the environment** | "If I lose track, the environment ran hot; it isn't my failure." | Void-State "must not imply blame"; *Blind Sense Guardian* |

### 7.3 The reactive, low-load stance

Inside an environment, Ryan proposes a **reactive** stance: respond to what arrives, without planning ahead (proactive) or replaying the past (reflective). The research gives this idea a precise form:

- **Capacity is limited.** Working memory and attention are limited. Problem-solving that loads working memory leaves less capacity for anything else (Sweller 1988).
- **Load decides what gets noticed.** In Lavie's load theory, *perceptual* load can block irrelevant input, while high *cognitive* load (holding plans and goals) makes people *more* distractible and worse at control (Lavie 2005). A person busy planning or ruminating has less control left to notice a quiet cue.
- **Open monitoring is trainable.** Meditation research separates focused attention from *open monitoring*, which is non-reactive awareness of whatever arises, without fixing on any one object (Lutz et al. 2008). It is the trained version of "noticing without grasping," the skill needed to catch a rest-state cue.
- **Absorption is not the enemy.** Flow, the absorbed state of high challenge matched by high skill, is linked to positive experience in work and leisure (Csikszentmihalyi & LeFevre 1989). The goal is to keep enough spare capacity to notice a cue, not to prevent absorption.
- **Wandering costs mood.** In a large experience-sampling study, people's minds wandered often, and they were less happy when they did (Killingsworth & Gilbert 2010). Reacting to the present moment, rather than drifting, is linked to better mood.

**Grade.** That load affects what is noticed is **Supported**. That a reactive, low-load stance makes a *rest-state cue* easier to notice is **Plausible**; this paper tests it (§11, R1).

### 7.4 The Exit Paradox and its resolution

**The paradox.**

- *Constant exit-vigilance* spoils enjoyment. Trying to keep watch over one's own mind can even backfire: under load, the monitoring process can bring up exactly what one tries to avoid (ironic processes; Wegner 1994).
- *Full absorption with no plan* is the trap that Checkpoint v1.1 C7 and the Mania–Immersion Equivalence warn about.

**Resolution: pre-commit once, then hand vigilance off.**

1. **Pre-commit once** (§4.1): span, cue, witness, and fact. An if–then plan made in advance runs later with little conscious effort (Gollwitzer 1999). Commitment devices are a well-studied way to bind a future self (Bryan, Karlan & Nelson 2010).
2. **Hand vigilance off** to the outside timer, the witness, and the system's circuit breakers. The user does not have to watch the door.
3. **Two legitimate endings, plus one safety valve:**
    - **Satisfaction.** "I'm satisfied" is a complete reason to leave.
    - **The witness's call.** Someone outside pulls the user back.
    - **The hard exit** is always available and never shamed.
4. **The rest state is a light, passive cue, not a task.** A single soft cue appears when the user slows down. It does not repeat, and it never asks "Do you want to exit?" again and again. **Enjoying the environment is allowed.**

The prototype follows this design. It has no repeated prompts. One soft line ("Rest is here. If you feel satisfied, that is an allowed ending.") appears once after a period of stillness. An "I'm satisfied" button sits alongside the witness call and the hard exit.

### 7.5 Counterpoints

- Some environments, such as games and creative work, *require* planning, so a purely reactive stance is not always possible. In those cases the stance applies to the *rest-state cue* only. Spare capacity is reserved for one signal.
- Handing off vigilance assumes a reliable witness or timer. Without one, the user is back to self-monitoring. The design therefore keeps the hard exit physical.
- "Satisfaction" can be manipulated by environments built never to satisfy, such as infinite feeds. This is one reason the external endings exist.

### 7.6 Testable prediction

- **R1.** Compared with participants instructed to "check regularly whether you should exit," participants who pre-commit once and hand vigilance to an outside cue will (a) report higher enjoyment, and (b) exit within ±10% of their planned span at least as often, with fewer unplanned overruns.

## 9. Integration: the rest state as a pause menu for the mind

Ryan describes the prototype in plain terms: *"This app is just a menu between playing in the environment and exiting it, which can be implemented in other apps or environments that require heavy human cognitive use."* He adds: *"This is like a pause menu that makes the user collect their thoughts as they dive into any environment they want, so they will be satisfied."*

The rest state is therefore best understood as a **pause menu for the mind**: a portable layer between *playing* and *exiting*. In it, a person collects their thoughts and then chooses one of three options:

- **Return** to the same environment,
- **Dive elsewhere** into another environment,
- **Exit** to the reality of origin.

### 9.1 Interface specification

```
STATES      Playing (host) → Rest ─┬→ Return  (back to the same environment)
                                  ├→ Dive    (another environment; exit rules travel along)
                                  └→ Exit → Limit → Read-back → Ground → Done → Closed

TRIGGERS    user slows (one soft cue, once) · "I'm satisfied" · witness call ·
            host circuit breaker (loop / danger threshold) · hard exit (always; never shamed)

COMPONENTS  reality watermark · witness light · breathing light · acceptance line ·
            limitation line · Loved Beyond anchor (opt-in) · neutral return room ·
            read-back · 5-4-3-2-1 · confirmable-fact check · closure line + 988 note

API         RestState.open({ witnessName, fact, allowReturn, environments, loved,
                             onReturn, onDive(id), onExit({reason}), onClose, onStateChange })
            RestState.satisfied() · RestState.hardExit() · RestState.callBack()
            RestState.close('return') · RestState.state · RestState.isOpen

RULES       no scores · no timers shown · no loss framing · no repeated prompts ·
            no forced camera motion · data stays on the device · hard exit is outside
            the host's control (hardware button, timer on another device, or a person)
```

### 9.2 Platform examples

| Platform | Pause-menu trigger | Rest space | Return / Dive / Exit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **VR world or game** | Pause button, a long pinch, the witness's call, a boundary event | The 3D riverbank (WebXR) | Resume the level / switch worlds / passthrough plus read-back |

## 11. Study design: "Rest-State Exit Pilot" (proposal only)

**Design.** A within-subjects, counterbalanced lab study with four conditions after a 12-minute engaging VR task:

- **A: Direct cut** to the room.
- **B: Blank pause** (a featureless grey screen, 2 minutes, then the room).
- **C: Rest state** (the riverbank with breathing light and watermark, 2 minutes, then the exit sequence).
- **D: Rest state + witness and limitation line** (C plus the Loved Beyond anchor if opted in, a live witness read-back, and the limitation line).

Sub-studies within the same sessions test R1 (reactive stance) and H2 (acceptance → exit).

**Participants.** 40–60 healthy adults. Excluded: history of psychosis, mania or hypomania, dissociative disorder, seizures, current acute distress, or prolonged grief disorder (Ryan's Checkpoint v1.1 exclusions). A State-Finder-style readiness check is used.

**Measures.**

- Time-to-reorientation (latency on a real-room probe).
- State depersonalization (CDS-State; Sierra & Berrios 2000).
- Simulator sickness (SSQ; Kennedy et al. 1993).
- Presence (Witmer & Singer 1998).
- Mood and restoration (short affect items).
- HRV during the rest period (expected to rise with paced breathing; Laborde et al. 2022).
- "Real vs. simulated?" probe accuracy (P2).
- Planned-versus-actual exit timing (P3, R1).
- Enjoyment.

**Predictions.** On disorientation and restoration, C and D should do better than A and B (P1, P4). D should do better than C on reorientation and exit adherence (P3, H2).

**Safety and ethics.** Ethics or IRB review, consent, stop rules, a 988 safety flow, and an environment-first debrief. Pre-registration on OSF with a synthetic-data analysis script. Ryan is PI; AI assistants are tools, not investigators.

## 12. Limitations

- **The files are conceptual.** Nearly all of Ryan's files describe themselves as conceptual. Their agreement shows coherence, not proof.
- **The new postulates are untested.** Acceptance as a reality cue (RS0), the Human–AI Exit Postulate, and the reactive stance draw on neighboring evidence. None has been tested directly.
- **Evidence varies in strength.** Virtual nature restores less than real nature. Fractal, color, and awe effects are context-dependent. The 5-4-3-2-1 sequence has no dedicated trial.
- **Product claims are a snapshot.** They were checked against vendor pages on one date (15 November 2026), and features change.
- **Mental-health framing.** Nothing here diagnoses or treats anyone. Mania language describes Ryan's research focus and a design risk. Activation signs belong with clinicians and supporters.
- **AI involvement.** AI assistants co-drafted several of the files, and an AI assistant wrote this synthesis. Shared-training bias is possible.
- **Faith content** is recorded as [Values]: honored, and not tested.
- **The prototype** is a demonstration. It has not been tested on a physical headset, and its witness link works only on one device.

## 13. Closing

Ryan asked whether a pause between worlds could become a doorway. His files already held most of the house: a rest box that is not failure, a sanctuary where nothing decays, an exit that ends on a blank screen and a real face, a read-back that says "Here," and a commons that calls rest *luoma*.

This paper adds a few things:

- a 3D place to hold that rest,
- a rule that the cue comes from outside,
- a menu that lets a person return, dive elsewhere, or go home,
- and a reminder, if the person wants one, that they are loved beyond the environment.

Whether the rest state becomes fundamental to future technology is a forecast. Whether it helps people come back clearly is a question a small study can answer, and the study is designed in §11.
