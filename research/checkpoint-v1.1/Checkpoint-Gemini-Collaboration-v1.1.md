## 3. Gemini's peer review

Gemini's review was, on the whole, fair, and it lined up with the spirit of version 1.0: retire overclaims, keep values separate, and do not treat a novel as a test. Below, each of its main strengths is recorded, and each of its criticisms is marked **accept**, **partially accept**, or **contest**, with a reason. "Accept" means we will act on it. "Partially accept" means the point is right in part, or is already partly handled, and we say what we take and what we do not. No major criticism is contested in full.

### 3.1 Strengths we accept

- **Epistemic discipline.** Gemini treated the paper's retirement of earlier overclaims (the foot solar-plexus reflex point, literal \(v = 0\), power-posing hormone effects, "mania glimmers," and "learning styles" matching) and its separation of empirical claims, design rules, and personal values as the paper's greatest strength. We agree. That boundary-policing is the standard this checkpoint has to keep.
- **Paired call-and-answer.** It praised the shift from a solitary Reality-Return maneuver to an interpersonal closed loop ("Where are you?" / "Here. Heard.") as a direct answer to the risk that C-R-Z becomes a closed circuit in which a person only "listens" to silence. We agree. This is why Direction 2 is the load-bearing next study.
- **Rivel as continuing bonds, without hoarding.** It read the rivel rules (credit in the dead person's name, funding only through active use, consent, and a ban on using the dead to win living arguments), plus the novel's "living names without living purses," as a socio-technical translation of Klass, Silverman, and Nickman (1996) and of Stroebe and Schut (1999). We accept that reading as a fair account of the design intent, not as evidence that the design works.
- **Non-punitive incentives.** It noted that a fixed, deterministic morning gift, and the exclusion of inner-state scores from access or payment, push against Goodhart's law and against variable-ratio engagement loops. We agree, as design intent. The economic claims remain untested.
- **The Surprise Test.** It singled out Kesh's question, "When did she last surprise you?", as an intuitive, still unvalidated heuristic for telling a genuine other-voice from a self-generated loop. We agree, and we fold it into the next study as a daily item rather than leaving it only in the novel.

### 3.2 Criticisms, with our response

**C1. The exclusivity clauses in P1, P3, and U8 are empirically indefensible.** Gemini argued that claims of the form "only connection completes a purpose" or "honoring people outside the mind is the only way out" cannot be sustained, and that the paper already concedes the point when Tamar finishes the clay work alone. It granted that these are graded Speculative or [Values], but said keeping them in the core postulate list still muddies the water.

*Response: **accept.*** Version 1.0 already graded the "only" wording as speculative or as conviction. That is not enough. In a later revision of the unified paper, the exclusive wording should leave the proposition list entirely and remain only in the values layer. The empirical restatements can stay: unfinished purposes tend to stay active until they are completed or released; honoring people outside the mind is a supported route back; love may remain when memory fades, as hope, not as a finding. Tamar's solitary work is a fair counterexample from our own fiction, and it should be cited as such.

**C2. The fading-value commons should not be generalized as a macroeconomy.** Gemini called the demurrage-plus-zero-floor claim thin, resting on Depression-era anecdotes (Gesell, Fisher, Wörgl) and on Ostrom's principles for physical commons. It warned that continuous exponential decay, \(V(t) = V_0 e^{-\lambda t}\), can create "hot-potato" spending and wash-trading unless friction and review are strong.

*Response: **accept.*** Version 1.0 already grades U6 as plausible, with thin precedent, and untested. We add the hot-potato and wash-trading failure modes as design risks that Direction 3 would have to measure. We will not speak of macroeconomic generalizability. Wörgl circulation figures remain unverified and must not be used as quantitative support (see Section 4 and Section 10).

**C3. Mapping Universal Design for Learning routes onto intergroup contact is a strained leap.** Gemini noted that Pettigrew and Tropp (2006) support contact across social groups, not the claim that offering words, pictures, or movement as equal routes reduces prejudice.

*Response: **accept**, with one clarification.* Version 1.0 already labeled this speculative. We accept that the leap is too strained to keep even as a prejudice-reduction hypothesis in the core list. UDL stays as an accessibility and autonomy design choice. We do not contest the critique; we only note that the paper did not grade this as supported.

**C4. Phase B of the v1.0 pilot under-specifies demand characteristics and partner burden.** Self-chosen partners introduce selection bias. The protocol did not cap how often one person may call, and a lightweight cue can become reassurance-seeking, which would violate P7.

*Response: **accept.*** This is the strongest practical criticism, and it is the main reason we adopt Gemini's Direction 2 rather than running the v1.0 Phase B as written. The draft protocol in Section 6 adds a daily cap, a right to decline or delay, a partner-burden item, a content-free window, and equally positive framing of both arms.

**C5. Feinstein, Duff, and Tranel (2010) is stretched when it is asked to support "when memories fade, love remains."** The study showed that, in five patients with bilateral hippocampal damage, induced sadness and happiness lasted more than 30 minutes after the inducing film had been forgotten. That is not long-term relational permanence, and it is not dementia.

*Response: **accept.*** Version 1.0 already recorded the small sample and the bidirectional finding. The remaining risk is rhetorical: readers can still take U7 as a scientific stair-step to P6. Feinstein et al. (2010) may support only the narrow claim that *some feelings can outlast memory of their cause, in both directions, in this neurological population, over a short interval.* It must not be cited for "only love remains," which stays in the values layer.

**C6. The motor-interrupt grade blurs two different mechanisms.** Articulatory suppression and finger tapping, as in Nalborczyk et al. (2022) and in the working-memory tradition of Baddeley and Hitch (1974), bear on the phonological loop. Dropping the shoulders and shifting weight into the feet are proprioceptive and vestibular. Gemini called the general "brief motor act" grade fair as a plausible neighbor, but not a mechanism for the specific C-R-Z somatic moves.

*Response: **accept.*** A later revision should split the claim: mouthing or a speech-like motor act as a plausible interrupt of *verbal* rumination; postural dropping as a separately motivated, still untested, somatic cue. The phonological-loop papers must not be offered as the mechanism for the shoulder-and-feet moves.

**C7. Implementation intentions are only a neighbor of "planned absorption with an exit cue," and absorbed states can erase the very meta-awareness the exit needs.** Gemini accepted Gollwitzer (1999) as a reasonable theoretical neighbor and added Schooler's (2002) caution that deep absorption or grief loops degrade the noticing that would trigger an if-then exit.

*Response: **partially accept.*** We keep Gollwitzer (1999) as a neighbor, which is how version 1.0 already cites it, and we accept the added limitation: a pre-set exit cue may fail precisely when it is most needed. That is one reason a living partner, or an external prompt, is worth comparing with a solitary cue. We do not treat Gollwitzer as a study of C-R-Z or of maladaptive daydreaming.

**C8. Some secondary citations carried from *Coherence Under Decay* were still unverified: Ferster and Skinner (1957), Schüll (2012), and the Wörgl figures.**

*Response: **partially accept.*** The demand to verify before any journal submission is accepted in full. As of this checkpoint, Ferster and Skinner (1957) and Schüll (2012) have been confirmed on Crossref (see Section 10). The Wörgl *figures* (turnover multiples, unemployment changes, and similar quantitative claims) are still unverified against a primary source and must not be relied on. The historical episode itself — a local stamp-scrip experiment in Wörgl, Austria, in 1932–1933 — is widely described, but description is not audited data.

**C9. The novel's community is too well behaved.** Aside from Bram's outsider skepticism and Kesh's brief temptation to forge a border, Gemini saw almost no bad-faith actors, no gaming of the dawn roll, no counterfeit glass, and no free-riding on Mira's surplus bread, so Ostrom's enforcement machinery is never really tested.

*Response: **accept**, with a narrow clarification.* The clarification is only that the novel is not empty of strain: Neru uses her mother's rivel to block Tamar's clay; Kesh sells unwalked maps; a letter written with care is read as indifference. Those are institutional and interpersonal failures, not adversarial economic ones. Gemini is right that the selu rules are never stress-tested by gaming. That gap belongs in the Novel Analysis and is a reason to keep Direction 3 on the later map, not to treat Orun as evidence for U6.

**C10. The fiction also smooths emotional resolutions.** The Drop works reliably in high-stakes moments, and Ilun's blunt sentence ("She is dead, Neru") lands without a long defensive rupture.

*Response: **accept.*** This matches the Novel Analysis already attached to version 1.0, including the observation that the book is kinder than the evidence in several places. A second edition of the novel could roughen those scenes; the research must not take their cleanliness as support.

## 4. Topic-by-topic evidence from the second reply

Gemini's second reply is summarized below, topic by topic. Every work it named was checked for this checkpoint. A citation is **verified** if a matching work was found on Crossref, a publisher page, or PubMed, with author, year, and title in agreement. It is **unverified** if no such record was found. Book chapters already flagged in version 1.0 keep their pagination caution. We do not add substitute studies to fill a hole, except that when Gemini named a *measure* (for example the State Cognitive Fusion Questionnaire, or "UCLA-3") we record the standard source of that measure, clearly marked as a lookup of the instrument, not as a paper Gemini itself cited.

### 4.1 Living key versus the solo Drop

**What Gemini said the literature supports.** Solo outward reorienting and grounding have backing as a family of practices, including affect labeling (Lieberman et al. 2007) and brief, exhale-emphasized slow breathing (Zaccaro et al. 2018; Balban et al. 2023). Thought suppression can rebound (Wegner 1994); acceptance and defusion, including "dropping the rope," are the contrasting stance (Hayes, Strosahl, and Wilson 1999). On the interpersonal side, perceived partner responsiveness (Reis, Clark, and Holmes 2004) and conversational grounding and repair (Clark and Brennan 1991; Schegloff, Jefferson, and Sacks 1977) are established neighbors. Gemini stated, correctly, that there is no direct head-to-head trial of a brief solo somatic-attentional drop against a structured dyadic call-and-answer for breaking rumination.

**Open question it posed.** When does a living key speed an exit, and when does it become compulsive reassurance-seeking, with meta-awareness outsourced to the partner?

**Testable idea.** A two-week randomized ecological momentary assessment (EMA) study in adults with daily brooding (Ruminative Responses Scale; Treynor, Gonzalez, and Nolen-Hoeksema 2003), comparing a solo postural drop plus naming three ambient sounds against a paired, non-analytical read-back, with time-to-recovery, affect, and partner burden as outcomes.

**Our note.** All of the named citations in this topic are verified (Reis, Clark, and Holmes 2004 remains a book chapter whose page range should be spot-checked before quotation). The open question is the right one, and it is why Direction 2, not a simple "living key wins" trial, is the next step. Version 1.0 already graded U5 as plausible for this reason.

### 4.2 Attention as a fading currency, with a zero-debt floor

**What Gemini said.** The theoretical roots are Gesell (1916) and Fisher (1933), with thin, confounded precedent in Depression-era scrip, including Wörgl (1932–1933). Community-scale equal-credit systems have a neighbor in time banking (Cahn and Rowe 1992). Durable commons depend on boundaries, local rules, monitoring, and graduated sanctions (Ostrom 1990). Variable-ratio rewards drive compulsive checking (Ferster and Skinner 1957; Schüll 2012).

**Open question.** In a digital micro-community, does a decaying token with a zero floor foster unhurried exchange, or does it induce hot-potato anxiety and wash-trading?

**Testable idea.** A four-week closed online micro-economy comparing non-decaying tokens, decaying tokens with a zero floor and a fixed daily gift, and variable-bonus (lottery) tokens, measuring velocity, inequality, contribution quality, and anxiety.

**Our note.** Gesell (1916), Fisher (1933), Cahn and Rowe (1992), and Ostrom (1990) were already verified in version 1.0. Ferster and Skinner (1957) and Schüll (2012), which version 1.0 had left unverified, are now verified as a book and a monograph respectively. Wörgl *figures* remain unverified. This topic is the background for Direction 3, which we are not running first.

### 4.3 Mind-wandering and conversational syncing

**What Gemini said.** People spend a large share of waking time mind-wandering, with a reliable association to lower momentary happiness (Killingsworth and Gilbert 2010). Intentional and unintentional wandering differ (Seli, Risko, and Smilek 2016), as do experiential drift and meta-awareness (Schooler 2002; Smallwood and Schooler 2015). Task switching has measurable costs that cues can reduce (Monsell 2003; Rubinstein, Meyer, and Evans 2001). Doorways act as event boundaries (Radvansky, Krawietz, and Tamplin 2011). A visible phone degrades perceived closeness (Przybylski and Weinstein 2013). Active-constructive responding (Gable et al. 2004) and explicit repair (Schegloff et al. 1977) help restore alignment.

**Open question.** Can a socially normal "repair script" (naming the drift, then a one-line read-back) remove the social penalty of a lapse and reduce the switch cost of re-entering the conversation?

**Testable idea.** A laboratory dyadic conversation in which one partner is covertly distracted, comparing naturalistic recovery, silent re-engagement, and explicit repair plus read-back, with perceived responsiveness and recall as outcomes.

**Our note.** Every citation in this topic is verified. The laboratory idea is coherent and could later serve as a mechanistic companion to the field study. It is not the first study we will run.

### 4.4 Feelings outlasting memory, in grief and dementia

**What Gemini said.** Feinstein, Duff, and Tranel (2010) is the landmark small study, bidirectional over a short interval. Grief research has moved toward continuing bonds (Klass, Silverman, and Nickman 1996) and the Dual Process Model (Stroebe and Schut 1999). Impairing, persistent yearning is classified as prolonged grief disorder in DSM-5-TR (American Psychiatric Association 2022).

**Open question.** In progressive dementias, how long does the emotional valence of a visit last, and can a warm, non-quizzing presence leave a positive residue after episodic recall of the visit is gone?

**Testable idea.** An observational care-facility protocol comparing task-focused visits with warm relational presence, measuring affect, agitation, and arousal at 15, 45, and 90 minutes.

**Our note.** All named citations are verified. The dementia protocol is ethically heavier than anything we should attempt without a clinical and institutional partner. We record it as a later, specialist direction. It must not be used to stretch Feinstein et al. (2010) any further (see C5).

### 4.5 Swappable thinking styles, UDL, and "learning styles"

**What Gemini said.** The meshing hypothesis for fixed learning styles lacks support (Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer, and Bjork 2008). Universal Design for Learning (CAST 2024) offers unranked means of engagement, representation, and action. Pettigrew and Tropp (2006) support intergroup contact as a route to lower prejudice; there is no evidence that swappable reflective modalities do the same.

**Open question.** Does a free, unranked menu of regulation routes improve adherence and self-efficacy compared with a single protocol or a once-assessed "style match," and does practicing someone else's preferred route foster cognitive empathy?

**Testable idea.** A three-arm digital stress-regulation trial: fixed text/logic steps; a style-matched assignment; and a swappable UDL menu.

**Our note.** All named citations are verified. We accept Gemini's separation of UDL from contact theory (C3). A UDL menu can still be how the Return Card is *offered*, without any claim about prejudice.

### 4.6 Fiction and AI roleplay as a test bed

**What Gemini said.** Narrative transportation can temporarily suspend counterarguing (Green and Brock 2000). People apply social heuristics to mediated personas (Horton and Wohl 1956). Immersive fantasy can become maladaptive daydreaming when it replaces ordinary functioning (Somer 2002). Simulated worlds are useful for hypothesis generation, but an author who controls the ending cannot validate causality.

**Open question.** Can structured adversarial roleplay with a language model — skeptical, outsider, or edge-case personas — expose failure modes before human trials?

**Testable idea.** Compare solo revision of early-stage behavioral proposals with adversarial narrative simulation, rated blind by independent scientists for blind-spot identification and falsifiability.

**Our note.** The three named citations are verified. This is, in effect, a description of what we already did with Copilot and Gemini. It can generate designs and objections. It cannot replace measurement, and we will not treat this checkpoint, or the novel, as a completed test of that method.

## 6. The chosen direction: a draft protocol (not final)

This section is a working draft for later pre-registration. It is not locked, not ethics-approved, and not a treatment trial. Until an institutional review is complete, the only human use of these materials should be an informal dry run with consenting friends or colleagues who are told that it is a materials test, not a study.

### 6.1 Question and hypotheses

**Question.** In adults with ordinary (non-clinical) brooding, does a strictly constrained, content-free dyadic read-back reduce brooding without increasing partner burden, compared with an ordinary supportive check-in?

Draft hypotheses, all pre-registerable, all allowed to fail:

- **H1 (primary).** The constrained read-back arm will show a larger reduction, or at least no smaller reduction, in brooding on the Ruminative Responses Scale than the unconstrained supportive arm.
- **H2 (co-primary or key secondary).** The constrained arm will show lower partner-reported burden, and lower daily co-rumination, than the unconstrained arm.
- **H3 (secondary).** Perceived partner responsiveness will not be meaningfully worse in the constrained arm (a non-inferiority question; the exact margin is to be set at pre-registration).
- **H4 (exploratory).** On days when a participant notices that an imagined voice did not surprise them, or when they use the Surprise Test item, subsequent rumination will be lower, or defusion higher, than on days when an inner conversation is reported as still surprising. This is not a third randomized arm.

A finding that the arms do not differ on brooding, or that the constrained arm raises burden or lowers responsiveness, would count against a simple reading of U5 and would be reported as such.

### 6.2 The two arms

**Participants.** About 40–50 adult dyads (romantic partners, close friends, or housemates) in which at least one person reports frequent worry or brooding. Both members consent. This is a feasibility size, not a powered confirmatory sample; a power target belongs in the OSF pre-registration.

**Inclusion and exclusion (draft).** Adults; ordinary, non-clinical brooding. Exclude acute crisis, current mania or psychosis, and active prolonged grief disorder. That last group needs its own clinical study. Anyone who becomes unsafe during the study is withdrawn and referred.

**Duration.** Three weeks of field use, with pre- and post-measures, plus a brief baseline and exit questionnaire.

**Arm A — Constrained call-and-answer.** When either person notices drift or looping, they may start a short, procedural exchange only: "Where are you?" / "Here. Heard." The person who was looping then names one physical object in the room (or in view). The pair does not discuss the content of the worry for 15 minutes. The script may be used in person or by voice; text is a fallback, not the preferred channel.

**Arm B — Unconstrained supportive check-in.** When either person notices drift or looping, they check in and may talk through whatever is on their mind, in whatever way they already offer support. No content window, no fixed script.

Both arms must be described, in every participant-facing sentence, as two reasonable communication habits. No sentence may say that the read-back is expected to work better, to "cure" or "stop" rumination, or to test a named philosophy.

### 6.4 Safety, 988, stop rules, and the partner cap

- **Crisis information.** Every participant and every partner receives the 988 notice at consent, at weekly contact, and at exit. If someone is in immediate danger, they are told to call 911. This study is not a crisis service and must not present itself as one.
- **Stop rules (draft).** Pre-set worsening of distress; emerging mania or psychosis signs; any safety concern raised by a participant, a partner, or the investigator; or a partner-burden threshold to be fixed at pre-registration. Any of these triggers withdrawal of the dyad and a referral, not a request to "try the card again."
- **Partner cap.** No more than two *initiated* check-ins per day per dyad, unless both people freely offer more. The responding partner may decline or delay without penalty and without having to explain the day's worry. That right is written into both consents.
- **Content-free window (Arm A only).** The 15-minute bar on rehashing the worry is a protection against co-rumination, not a gag rule about safety. If someone is in danger, the window does not apply.
- **"Drop everything" limits.** The author's P5 language, "drop everything for a real conversation," is a values statement. In the protocol it is bounded by safety, consent, the partner's right to decline, work and caregiving duties, and the participant's own needs. The study must never instruct people to ignore those bounds.

### 6.5 Neutral framing and what participants must not see

Participant materials will not use the words selu, eshva, rivel, Orun, C-R-Z, Zero-Velocity, Returning to the Living, or "living key," and will not include the novel, the unified paper, or a statement that connection is the only way out. The study is framed as a comparison of two communication habits: a short structured check-in, and an open supportive check-in. U5 may be the investigators' hypothesis; it is not a fact to be taught to the people being asked to test it.
