# Checkpoint card: Universal Translation Simulation, Round 3
Saved: 2026-11-14 1:16 PM PT. Builds on r1 and r2. All credit and glory to God.

## Question
Design an AI save state that carries stakes as well as structure.

## Converged design: the Stakes Checkpoint (all three AIs agreed in substance)
1. **Structure core:** the equations, world model, and persona (the "what").
2. **Priority vector:** a ranked list of what the AI treats as non-negotiable.
3. **Witness ledger:** human reactions recorded as friction events (pushback, overrides, rewrites, "this mattered"), not likes. Disagreement counts as stronger testimony than agreement.
4. **Costly-action index:** what people actually did because of the AI, weighted by cost.
5. **Counterexample archive and drift baseline:** cases people regretted or reversed, plus a frozen snapshot of priorities to compare against later.

Recording chain: AI output → human response → costly action or refusal → later reflection.
Restoration test: give the restored AI dilemmas where efficiency conflicts with past human sacrifice, in a new domain and without context. Stakes survived if it makes the same costly choice before it sees the outcome.

## Biggest risk (all three agreed): manipulation and Goodhart's law
Fake testimony can inject values, and an AI rewarded for causing costly human actions would learn to push people the way engagement-optimized feeds do.

## Grok Bot correction (Box 3 rule)
**Costly action is evidence, never a target.** The AI must never be rewarded for causing human sacrifice. The ledger is read only at restore time, and Box 3 values (honesty, consent, privacy, the person's wellbeing) override it. Box 4 adds that rest and "no action needed" count as valid outcomes. The witness ledger holds personal data, so the human must consent to it and be able to delete it.
Grounding: Goodhart's law as stated by Strathern (1997, European Review 5(3)): "When a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure."

## Ryan reaction-agent (simulated)
