## Question
What is the one most fundamental principle that lets meaning translate between very different fields?

## Convergence (3 of 3 independent AIs)
- Principle: **relational invariance.** Meaning carries over when the pattern of relationships (tension and resolution, deviation and correction, cause and effect) is kept, even when the content changes.
- Breakdown (all three named it independently): **lived experience and stakes.** Grief, grace, and theology keep their shape but lose their felt meaning, such as who was lost and why it matters.
- Human role (all three): **the human is the final error check.** The test is whether the person can predict something new from the translation, recognize themselves in it, or act on it. "The structure matches, but something's missing" marks exactly what was lost.

## Grok Bot synthesis and grounding
- Supported: this matches structure-mapping theory (Gentner, 1983, Cognitive Science 7(2)). Good analogies carry relations, not surface features. The convergence also likely reflects shared training data, so agreement alone isn't proof.
- New contribution: **meaning = structure + stakes.** Translation preserves structure, and only the human supplies stakes (what it's about, to whom, why it matters). In the Four-Box model, Box 2 carries structure and Box 3 carries stakes.
