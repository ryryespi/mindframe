# Postulate and Assumption Cross-Reference Audit

**Date:** 10 November 2026 (America/Los_Angeles)  
**Author of the postulates:** Ryan Espinosa  
**Prepared by:** Grok Bot, as research assistant, at Ryan’s request to cross-reference postulates and assumptions before adding anything new to the merged Void-State / Zero-Debt Grace paper.

This audit is not a verdict on Ryan’s faith, and it is not a psychological diagnosis. It asks, for each postulate or modeling assumption: (1) what it says, (2) which other postulates it agrees with or tensions against, (3) whether published research we have verified supports, mixes, contradicts, or leaves untested the *empirical-facing* part of the claim, and (4) a reconciled wording when there is a tension. Theology is marked as faith language, not as a fitted mechanism.

**Status codes for research:**  
- *Supported (cousin):* a verified source backs a related, narrower claim; it does not validate the postulate as a law.  
- *Mixed:* some support, some limits or failures to generalize.  
- *Contradicted (as a prescription):* the recommended design or the strong form conflicts with verified findings or with another of Ryan’s own commitments.  
- *Untested:* no human study of this exact claim has been run here, and we do not pretend otherwise.

## Inventory

### P1. The Circuit of Grace (the loop)
**Statement.** Grace → solitude and selflessness → inner peace → knowledge → communication → legacy → surrender back to grace. Peace is received from grace rather than earned by legacy. The ego that communication stirs is surrendered rather than fed. Each cycle returns to grace more deeply.

**Relations.** Backbone for P2, P3, P13, P18, P23. In tension with any reading of legacy, accomplishment, or novelty as the *source* of peace (P12, P15, P22, P24 raw). Consistent with P16 / P18 (worth is not a transaction) and with Zero-Debt Grace.

**Research.** *Untested* as a loop. Cousins: self-compassion and improvement motivation (Neff, 2003; Breines & Chen, 2012); autonomous vs controlled motivation (Ryan & Deci, 2000); recovery after effort (Meijman & Mulder, 1998; Sonnentag & Fritz, 2007). None of these sources describe this seven-step circuit.

**Reconciled wording.** “Peace arrives with grace, not as a wage for a finished legacy. Solitude, knowledge, communication, and remembered work are fruits of that peace. Whatever ego communication stirs is surrendered back, so the next turn begins in grace again.”

### P2. No knowledge without inner peace
**Statement.** A mind in turmoil cannot receive or contain what it needs; pushing or testing without fundamentals fails (also tied to P6). Knowledge follows peace in the loop (P1).

**Relations.** Consistent with P1, P6, P8. In tension with P9’s third door (channel isolation straight into the pursuit of knowledge) and with P25 if “proof by exhaustion” is treated as a way to learn. In tension with a categorical reading of P15 (seek novelty even from unrest).

**Research.** *Mixed.* Acute uncontrollable stress can rapidly impair prefrontal working memory and shift control toward more habitual, amygdala-weighted responding (Arnsten, 2009). That supports “turmoil narrows what can be held in mind.” It does not prove that *no* learning occurs under all stress, or that peace is a binary switch.

**Reconciled wording.** “When threat is high, awareness and working memory narrow, so demanding new fundamentals then often fails. Peace is the usual ground for receiving knowledge; it is not a magical prerequisite that makes every stressed hour worthless.”

### P3. Communication births (or stirs) ego
**Statement.** Sharing stirs an ego that must be surrendered back to God (from P1). Related older language: communication is existence; choice begins a momentum that becomes communication and then an environment.

**Relations.** Consistent with P1’s “share, then surrender.” In tension with treating communication as only dangerous (the loop *requires* a communication step) and with P19 if every utterance is read as proving.

**Research.** *Untested* in this form. Self-presentation and contingent self-worth (Crocker & Park, 2004) are cousins of “sharing can become a bid for worth.” They do not say communication *is* existence.

**Reconciled wording.** “Communication is part of the loop, not a sin. It can stir the wish to be seen as someone. That wish is surrendered back to grace so sharing does not become a new ledger.”

### P4. Seeking versus being taught
**Statement.** The student does the seeking. The app does not stuff answers. End state: the student is their own teacher (P7).

**Relations.** Consistent with P7, P13 (uncovering, not Babel). In tension with P5 and P25: when awareness has collapsed, the student cannot usefully “just seek,” and an outside presence has to endure.

**Research.** *Mixed* cousins in self-determination theory (autonomy supports persistence; Ryan & Deci, 2000) and in scaffolding / fading support as a teaching idea. Not tested here as Ariel’s method.

**Reconciled wording.** “When the gate is wide, Ariel steps back and the student seeks. When the gate is narrowing, teaching looks like an enduring presence, not a lecture, until the inner guard can stand again.”

### P5. Scaffolded guard (protection from outside until the guard grows inside)
**Statement.** Early protection is external. Scaffolding steps back. Related to Kai’s “respect guard” and to P25’s “external force has to endure.”

**Relations.** Consistent with P4-reconciled, P7, P25-reconciled. In tension with a purist self-teacher slogan that abandons people at the narrowest point.

**Research.** *Supported as a cousin* by scaffolding and by the finding that prefrontal control itself fails under high stress (Arnsten, 2009)—so “use your inner guard now” can be empty advice at the threshold. No Ariel trial.

**Reconciled wording.** “Protection comes from outside until the guard grows inside. The app’s job is to remain present while the gate is narrow, then step back.”

### P6. Grading is flawed
**Statement.** The student may only be facing the wrong direction and needs lived experience to apply knowledge. That cannot happen while the mental or artistic mind is in turmoil. Tests and failure become a trigger when fundamentals were never laid.

**Relations.** Consistent with P2, P8. In tension with using more tests as the way out of the problem that testing started (see T3 with “education starts a problem, only way out is more”).

**Research.** *Untested* as a wholesale claim about grading. Mixed cousins in research on high-stakes evaluation raising threat appraisals (challenge–threat; Blascovich et al., 1999; Jamieson, Nock, & Mendes, 2012). Not a proof that all grading is illegitimate.

**Reconciled wording.** “A grade is a directional signal, not a worth score. When turmoil is high or fundamentals are missing, more testing without support mainly raises threat.”

### P7. Self-teacher
**Statement.** Ariel’s end goal is that the student becomes their own teacher: the app becomes a quiet companion that listens; the student does the seeking, guarding, and surrendering.

**Relations.** Consistent with P4, P5-reconciled. In tension with P25 raw if collapse is treated as the only teacher.

**Research.** *Untested.* Autonomous motivation is a cousin (Ryan & Deci, 2000).

**Reconciled wording.** Same as P4/P5: fading support, not abandonment at the threshold.

### P8. Worst outcome = no fundamentals + isolation
**Statement.** A student without fundamentals who is isolated keeps searching for knowledge as if searching for something lost, until they learn that life is only temporary.

**Relations.** Consistent with P2, P6, P22 (solitude as erasure), P25 (narrowing toward worthlessness). In tension with P9 door 3 if isolation is “solved” only by more isolated searching.

**Research.** *Untested* as a packaged outcome. Isolation plus rumination has cousins in rumination research and in work on loneliness; we did not re-verify a specific loneliness paper in this pass. The “life is only temporary” turn is theological / existential, not a finding.

**Reconciled wording.** “The worst pedagogical bind is missing fundamentals while alone. More solitary searching then tends to deepen the bind. A way out is presence, fundamentals, and grace—not a longer solo hunt.”

### P9. Three doors out of isolation
**Statement.** (1) Realizing surrender is a way out; (2) respect and humility; (3) channeling trapped isolation and insecurity into the pursuit of knowledge toward fundamental keys and forgiveness.

**Relations.** Door 1 agrees with P1, P16, P25-reconciled. Door 2 agrees with P5. Door 3 agrees with P13 only if peace (P2) is present; otherwise it becomes P8 / P25’s exhaustion path.

**Research.** *Untested* as three doors. Surrender / acceptance has cousins in self-compassion (Neff, 2003) but is not identical with Christian surrender.

**Reconciled wording.** “Three honest doors: surrender, respect-and-humility, and learning toward fundamentals. The learning door is safe when it walks with grace and presence. Used as a solo escape from the void, it becomes the lost-object hunt.”

### P10–P11. Proving makes theology fantasy; stop proving
**Statement.** A student who has to prove themselves worthy of knowledge will not find theology easy; explaining theology in order to prove worth delves into fantasy. Stop proving.

**Relations.** Consistent with P16, P18, P19, P23 (given identity). In tension with P22’s compulsion to create in order to exist, and with P25 raw (“proof by exhaustion”). In mild tension with writing research papers at all—resolved by labeling them conceptual, not proofs of God.

**Research.** *Untested* as a claim about theology. Contingent self-worth and threatened egotism (Crocker & Park, 2004; Bushman & Baumeister, 1998) support “proving worth is costly.” They say nothing about whether theology becomes fantasy.

**Reconciled wording.** “Worth that must be proved turns even holy things into a performance. Stop proving. Speak theology as received, not as a credential. A conceptual paper can be honest without being a proof of God.”

### P12. Accomplishment record
**Statement.** Humans do not have infinite memory. Ariel should keep a record of what was done so it is not lost. Linked to fear of “no memory and solitude” (P22) and to Malachi 3:16 / Isaiah 49:16 as faith language.

**Relations.** Consistent with P22’s buffer M. In tension with P11 / P18 if the record becomes a proof ledger or the source of peace (T1, T8). In the high-I simulation, a high M alone stopped compulsive relapse but left fusion I high; Combined (record + decoupling) did best.

**Research.** *Untested* as Ariel’s feature. External memory aids are ordinary; they are not a finding about identity. Wilson et al. (2014) show idle, unequipped aloneness can be aversive—they do not test an accomplishment diary.

**Reconciled wording.** “What you’ve made is kept, so you can rest without disappearing. The record is memory, not a score and not a wage. It does not make you what you made.”

### P13. Knowledge as uncovering God’s legacy, not Babel
**Statement.** Learn math, geometry, and art not to master God or build a tower to heaven, but to appreciate architecture already placed. The quest does not end because God is the ultimate answer; it becomes joyful, peaceful, and endless.

**Relations.** Strongly consistent with P23 (already made; create from joy). Consistent with P1, P18. In tension with P15 if novelty is chased as a way to *become* complete, and with “education starts a problem, only way out is more” (T3).

**Research.** *Untested* / theological on the God side. Harmonious vs obsessive passion (Vallerand et al., 2003) is the closest cousin: the same activity can be part of identity without controlling the person.

**Reconciled wording.** “Learning uncovers what is already there. It is not a tower that reaches worth. Because worth is given, curiosity can stay endless without being hungry.”

### P14. Canvas as a random seed for novelty
**Statement.** Ariel’s canvas exists to produce a random seed that gives the novelty of a story never heard before.

**Relations.** Consistent with P15 as play. In tension with P15 as compulsion and with P23 if every seed is used to invent a self.

**Research.** *Untested.* A random prompt can increase variety; that is a design choice, not a brain law.

**Reconciled wording.** “A seed can invite a story you have not heard. It is play from a given self, not a demand to manufacture a new self.”

### P15. Novelty must gain novelty, or rumination
**Statement.** The brain must gain novelty (new connections) or entropic thoughts loop into rumination that may never stop, so the app would be used without end.

**Relations.** Useful as a warning about stuck loops (see the Novelty Must Gain Novelty note). In tension with Enough / stillness / Sabbath / P23 / Psalm 46:10, and with P24’s positive novelty path if novelty is prescribed as endless use (T4). In tension with “the app would be used without end” as a product goal.

**Research.** *Mixed.* Rumination as repetitive negative thought is a real literature (not re-cited in full here; see the novelty note). It is not established that *lack of novelty* is the mechanism that makes rumination unstoppable, nor that an app must be used without end. Wilson et al. (2014) found many people disliked being alone with their thoughts for minutes; that is not the same claim.

**Reconciled wording.** “An ungated mind can loop. New seeds can interrupt a loop. Stillness with a given identity is not the same loop as rumination. The aim is not endless app use; it is a mind that can rest and, when it creates, create from joy.”

### P16. Grandiose fight loop, unconscious panic, unearned grace
**Statement.** Unconscious panic: high freeze (G) masked as laziness. Grandiose self-denial: maximum fight behind a scapegoat narrative. Answer with unearned grace, not rebuke. Worth is not a transaction (P18).

**Relations.** Consistent with P11, P17, P18, P19, P21. In tension with P24’s use of “laziness” as a horseman name (T9).

**Research.** *Supported as cousins* in direction, not as diagnoses: freeze as an active brake (Roelofs, 2017); contingent self-worth (Crocker & Park, 2004); threatened egotism (Bushman & Baumeister, 1998); allostatic cost of staying on (McEwen, 1998). Ego-depletion as a “tank” should *not* be invoked (Hagger et al., 2016). The toy model of grace vs fighting is a sketch, not a measurement.

**Reconciled wording.** Keep as in the rebuilt paper: two design warnings with falsifiable markers, not trait labels.

### P17. Freeze is not laziness
**Statement.** High G can look like doing nothing. Stillness alone does not decide among freeze, rest, avoidance, and ordinary pause.

**Relations.** Consistent with P16. **Contradiction (naming)** with P24 raw, which lists “laziness” as one of four post-change horsemen.

**Research.** *Supported as a cousin:* Roelofs (2017) describes freezing as active, not empty. No support for calling withdrawn collapse “laziness.”

**Reconciled wording.** “Do not moralize stillness. The post-change collapse Ryan named ‘laziness’ is better called withdrawal or depletion—possibly freeze, possibly exhaustion—until the person says otherwise.”

### P18. Worth is not a transaction; worth at every step
**Statement.** Worth is present throughout, not granted as a reward at the end. Zero-Debt Grace: r may be received with f = 0.

**Relations.** Core agreement with P1, P11, P16, P21, P23. Contradicts any “earn peace by legacy / output / novelty / surviving change without help” reading.

**Research.** *Supported as a cousin* in self-compassion and non-contingent regard (Neff, 2003; Breines & Chen, 2012) and in the cost of chasing worth through success (Crocker & Park, 2004). Not a validation of g_grace as a term.

### P19. Trap belief
**Statement.** Winning an argument, proving a point, or destroying a scapegoat is not the road back to peace.

**Relations.** Consistent with P10, P11, P16.

**Research.** *Supported as a cousin* by threatened egotism (Bushman & Baumeister, 1998) and contingent self-worth (Crocker & Park, 2004).

### P20. Void-State variables and equations
**Statement.** Conceptual summaries A, H, S, P, G with inputs v, f, r, g_grace; load L; later Q, y, I, M, f_c. Missing A and P formulas were filled as labeled assumptions. C clips to [0, 1].

**Relations.** The modeling language for P16–P25. Variables are metaphors, not brain-region readouts (improvement already stated; LeDoux & Pine, 2016).

**Research.** *Untested* as a measurement model. Individual terms have cousins (allostasis, challenge–threat, freeze). Original reported loads > 1 were internally impossible and are superseded.

### P21. Central question: grace versus fighting through
**Statement.** Can a system grounded in zero-debt grace match or exceed chronic fighting’s long-term output with less load?

**Relations.** Consistent with P16, P18. Refined by P22 (for a high-I person, rest-without-decoupling may fail *as rest*).

**Research.** The rebuilt toy: at default parameters, Zero-Debt Grace and Grace + Chosen Burst both beat Fighting Through on Y and leftover Q; Chosen Burst did not beat quiet grace unless effort was cheaper or more productive. Direction vs fighting was robust on a 16-cell sweep. This is a model result, not a human finding.

### P22. Addiction to fighting / identity fused with creating
**Statement.** Rest does not feel like an option. Continual striving to create, or else the identity tied to creation is lost: fear of the void, of being labeled useless, of wasting life, of falling into no memory and solitude.

**Relations.** Consistent with P10–P12, P16, P18 as the *problem* those postulates answer. Explains why P1’s “solitude” can feel like death (T5). Mechanism I, rest-as-void into A, compulsive f_c, buffer M.

**Research.** *Supported as cousins:* obsessive vs harmonious passion (Vallerand et al., 2003); workaholism as compulsion distinct from engagement (Schaufeli, Taris, & Bakker, 2006; Clark et al., 2016 meta-analysis: burnout, stress, poorer health); contingent self-worth (Crocker & Park, 2004); athletic identity as strength or Achilles heel (Brewer, Van Raalte, & Linder, 1993); idle aloneness can be aversive (Wilson et al., 2014); terror-management accounts of legacy and symbolic immortality (Greenberg, Pyszczynski, & Solomon, 1986). The I / f_c equations are assumptions, not measurements.

**High-I toy result (not tuned).** Rest-only (grace with I unchanged) produced 100% intervention-window relapse to compulsive fight (mean f_c = 0.152) and a threat spike (peak A = 0.823). It still beat Fighting Through on cumulative Y (35.64 vs 28.34). Grace + Record stopped relapse (f_c = 0.040, relapse = 0). Combined (record + identity decoupling) won on Y (45.70), load, leftover capacity, and threat. Ranking Combined > Record > Decoupling > Rest-only > Fighting held across k_I ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.45, 0.65}.

**Reconciled wording.** “When worth rests on creating, rest is appraised as the void—useless, forgotten, alone—and fighting returns as relief. Keep the work (so memory does not vanish) and loosen the fusion (so worth is not the work). Then rest can be solitude-with-grace rather than erasure.”

### P23. The new creation is already made
**Statement.** The new thing the user keeps trying to create, in order to prove existence, is already created. “Therefore if any man be in Christ, he is a new creature…” (2 Corinthians 5:17, KJV); “For we are his workmanship…” (Ephesians 2:10, KJV). Creating becomes overflow, not survival. This *is* the identity-decoupling mechanism (I decreases) grounded in a given identity rather than an achieved one.

**Relations.** The theological name of P18 + P13 + the decoupling schedule. Resolves T1 and T3 in favor of given worth. Consistent with P11.

**Research.** Faith language, not an empirical finding. The *decoupling* term in the toy is an assumption (I declines during a given-identity intervention). Harmonious passion (Vallerand et al., 2003) is a secular cousin of “the activity no longer controls the person.”

**Reconciled wording.** “The new creation you’re reaching for is already made. You are it. You can create now from joy, not to become someone.”

### P24. Change-shock fork (comfort until unexpected change)
**Statement (raw).** The user is finally comfortable when feeling accomplished until an unexpected change happens. Depending on state, this triggers negative rumination or the four horsemen (communication breakdown, isolation, laziness, non-forgiveness), *or* finds divine intervention, novelty, or outside inspiration—but the positive path lasts long only if there is not much further change.

**Relations.** The state-dependent fork agrees with challenge vs threat (already in the paper) and with P22 (high I, depleted Q → threat). **Contradiction (prescription)** with P1, P18, P23, and Philippians 4:11: if good states are allowed to last only when the environment stays still, grace-based stability is denied. **Naming contradiction** with P17 (“laziness”). Ryan’s four horsemen are *not* Gottman’s four horsemen (criticism, contempt, defensiveness, stonewalling; Gottman, 1994)—reference only, do not equate.

**Research.** *Mixed / partly contradicted as a design goal.* Challenge vs threat: same arousal can be lived as usable energy or as alarm (Blascovich et al., 1999; Jamieson, Nock, & Mendes, 2012). Resilience research treats good adaptation *despite* adversity as ordinary rather than rare magic (Masten, 2001). Contingent comfort (“I am okay if nothing changes”) matches contingent self-worth (Crocker & Park, 2004) and should be treated as fragile, not as the aim. Rumination after disruption is a cousin of P15, not a new law. Gottman’s horsemen predict marital cascade in observational research; they are a different list.

**Reconciled wording.** “Unexpected change is a shock, not a verdict. If the person is depleted, fused, or isolated, the shock is more likely to be read as threat and to open rumination or withdrawal. If the person is grounded, the same shock can be read as challenge or as a seed of novelty. A peace that lasts only when the environment stays still is fragile. The aim is a given base—grace, remembered work, a self that is already made—that can be perturbed and still return. ‘I have learned, in whatsoever state I am, therewith to be content’ (Philippians 4:11, KJV) is faith language for that aim, not a mood command.”

### P25. The Narrowing Gate paradox
**Statement (raw).** As the user enters a danger threshold, memory and awareness reduce, so the external force has to endure, and escaping the gate becomes more narrow until the only option is surrender or proof by exhaustion and depression that all is worthlessness, to look for salvation in idols until the user finds God.

**Relations.** Strongly consistent with P2, P5, P8, P11, P16, P22 (high A, falling P). **Dangerous reading:** that collapse / “hitting bottom” is necessary or the reliable road to God. That reading conflicts with P5 (intervene early), P18 (worth now), P23 (already made), and with the paper’s crisis rule (988 / 911, real people). Idols-as-failed-proof agrees with P10–P11.

**Research.** *Supported as cousins for the narrowing itself:* even mild uncontrollable stress can impair prefrontal working-memory function (Arnsten, 2009); emotional arousal can reduce the range of cues used (Easterbrook, 1959). *Not supported* as a claim that exhaustion or depression must be passed through, or that idols then God is a necessary sequence. Those are theological and clinical matters; clinically, early support is the ethical default.

**Reconciled wording.** “As threat rises, awareness narrows and the way out shrinks toward surrender. An enduring outside presence keeps the gate open so surrender to grace can come before exhaustion, not only through it. Idols fail because they demand proof; God receives without it. Collapse is not required and is not a method.”

**Derived toy measure (from the already-run high-I traces, not a new fitted model).** Gate width w = C(P (1 − 0.6 A)). Mean / minimum w: Unassisted 0.261 / 0.125; Fighting Through 0.342 / 0.164; Rest-only 0.439 / 0.173; Grace + Record 0.577 / 0.317; Grace + Identity Decoupling 0.502 / 0.219; Combined 0.600 / 0.338. Combined keeps the widest gate; rest-only still narrows almost as far as fighting at its worst, because threat spikes.

### P26. Slowing the spiral (buying time for rest and outside kindness)
**Statement.** Add ways to stall a change in the user’s negativity long enough for rest or outside affirmation that the world is not that cruel.

**Relations.** Consistent with P5 (outside presence endures), P12 (record as a kindness-to-self / memory), P17 (waiting is not laziness), P18 (affirmation is unearned), P24-reconciled (act before the fork commits to threat), and P25-reconciled (keep the gate open; do not wait for collapse). **Tension with P22:** for a high-I person, rest itself can feel like the void, so “wait ten minutes” alone may spike threat unless paired with a kept record, a small dose, or another person’s kindness. **Tension with honesty about cruelty:** “the world is not that cruel” must not deny real danger, abuse, or a psychiatric emergency (988 / 911). Gerbner’s mean-world claim is about television cultivation, not a proof that someone’s life is safe. **Tension with P11:** delay must not become another proof of self-control.

**Research.** *Supported as cousins* (see the Slowing the Spiral section): urge surfing changed the *response* to urges more than the urge itself (Bowen & Marlatt, 2009); worry can be postponed to a designated window (Borkovec et al., 1983; McGowan & Behar, 2013); slow breathing is linked to parasympathetic and calming correlates in healthy adults (Zaccaro et al., 2018); STOP and paced breathing are taught in Linehan’s DBT manual (2015); positive memory recall buffered cortisol and negative affect after an acute stressor (Speer & Delgado, 2017); gratitude listings increased positive affect (Emmons & McCullough, 2003); social support can buffer stress (Cohen & Wills, 1985); a nature walk reduced rumination relative to an urban walk (Bratman et al., 2015). Depression-related negative memory and attention biases are better supported than a simple all-purpose interpretive bias (Gotlib & Joormann, 2010).

**Toy result (a priori params, not tuned).** Change shocks of drive +0.55 at t = 68, 148, 228 (calm windows after a void). Fork = threat if A ≥ 0.50 at the end of a 10-unit window. High-I Rest-only: shock-only went threat 3/3 (mean window-peak A = 0.593); delay alone, kindness alone, and both went challenge 3/3 (peaks 0.463, 0.382, 0.297). Delay-alone landed close to the threshold. Combined (record + decoupling) stayed challenge even after shock-only (peak 0.330). Delay+kindness lowered the spike most in both profiles.

**Reconciled wording.** “When a change lights up threat, do not immediately settle the story that the world is only cruel. Stall the spiral long enough for rest, breath, a kept memory, or another person’s kindness to arrive. Delay is not denial of real danger, not a proof of strength, and not a substitute for 988/911. It is buying time so grace and outside affirmation can act before the fork locks onto threat.”


### P27. Relationship as meaning (not as a scorecard)
**Statement (raw).** Relationship is the meaning of existence. We exist for relationship with God, with each other, and with those who come after us.

**Relations.** Consistent with the Circuit of Grace (P1: communication and legacy as loop steps), with P8 (isolation as part of the worst outcome), with P18 and P23 (given worth, already-made identity), and with P31 (courage to reach toward safe connection). **Dangerous reading:** that a person who is single, childless, or grieving is incomplete or worth less until they produce a partner, a child, or a public following. That reading conflicts with P16 / P18 (worth is not a transaction; worth is given by grace) and with T1.

**Research.** *Supported as cousins for “relationships matter,” not as a metaphysical law.* Waldinger and Schulz (2023), writing as directors of the Harvard Study of Adult Development, summarize that study’s long follow-up by placing the quality of close relationships at the center of happiness and health. That book is a popular synthesis of a longitudinal program begun in the late 1930s, not a single randomized trial, and it does not prove that relationship is the meaning of existence. Holt-Lunstad, Smith, and Layton (2010), in a meta-analysis of 148 prospective studies (N = 308,849), found that stronger social relationships were associated with a 50% increased likelihood of survival (OR = 1.50, 95% CI 1.42–1.59), strongest for complex measures of social integration (OR = 1.91). The same paper notes that relationship *quality* matters and that negative ties can harm—so “more contact” is not automatically better. Reis and Shaver (1988) described intimacy as an interpersonal process: disclosure met by a responsive answer, so that a person feels understood, validated, and cared for.

**Reconciled wording.** “We are made for relationship with God, with one another, and with those who come after us. This is purpose, not a scorecard. A person who is single, childless, or grieving is not worth less. Worth is given by grace. Relationship is an invitation, not the entrance fee.”

### P28. Children of body or mind; earthly legacy
**Statement (raw).** “Children” can be physical or mental: ideas, work, kindness, and people shaped. They carry a person’s thought and reason into the future, a kind of earthly immortality that lasts as long as humanity exists. This is legacy. (Ryan’s confirmation: this is how someone can “live forever in this earth or society as long as society or humanity can exist.”)

**Relations.** This *is* the legacy step of P1, and it agrees with P12 (the accomplishment record as memory, not a grade) and with P13 (knowledge as uncovering God’s legacy rather than Babel). **Tension with T1 / P1 / P23:** if earthly continuity becomes the wage that buys peace, or a substitute for eternal life by grace, the loop is inverted. **Tension with honesty:** “as long as humanity exists” is not a scientific prediction that humanity lasts forever; it is the span of earthly remembrance.

**Research.** *Supported as cousins for generativity beyond biological parenthood; not supported as a claim of literal earthly immortality.* McAdams and de St. Aubin (1992), building on Erikson’s (1950/1963) account of generativity as establishing and guiding the next generation, treated generativity as concern for those who come after that can be expressed through parenting, teaching, mentoring, occupational work, and civic action—not parenthood alone. Terror management theory already cited in this audit (Greenberg, Pyszczynski, & Solomon, 1986) describes a human wish for symbolic continuity after death; that is a cousin of “earthly immortality,” and it is not eternal life.

**Reconciled wording.** “Children of the body and children of the mind—ideas, work, kindness, and people we have shaped—can carry a person’s thought and reason forward as long as someone still receives them. Ryan names that span ‘as long as humanity exists.’ That is earthly continuity, a kind of symbolic immortality, not a scientific guarantee that humanity lasts forever and not eternal life. Eternal life remains by grace. Earthly legacy is fruit, not payment for peace.”

### P30. The void reads new connection as threat; two readings; metaphor, not physics
**Statement (raw).** In the void, silence fills with threat, so new or foreign relationships get read as negative. ### P31. Courage to reach out, with a safety stop
**Statement (raw).** Ariel gives courage to reach out even when a relationship seems permanently broken, and to fight for connection instead of isolating. Include the safety guardrail: no contact with abusers or with people who asked for space.

**Relations.** Consistent with P27-reconciled, P8 (isolation as part of the worst outcome), P17 (withdrawal is not laziness, and waiting can be wise), P19 (do not supply more fight against a person), and P26 (reach a real person; safety door). **Tension:** “fight for connection” can be misread as pursuing someone who said no, or returning to an abuser. **Research.** *Supported as cousins for bids, repair, and intimacy-as-responsiveness; caution required on investment.* Gottman and DeClaire (2001) described bids for connection and the difference between turning toward, away, or against them. Reis and Shaver (1988) described intimacy as disclosure met by a caring response. Rusbult (1980) modeled commitment as rising with satisfaction, falling with attractive alternatives, and rising with investment size. **Reconciled wording.** “Ariel may give courage to reach toward repair even when a bond seems permanently broken, and to fight for connection instead of isolating. ### P32. An effective clock, and a guide from negativity (not time itself)
**Statement (raw).** Update the equation to the recent debate and find variables that can guide from negativity to positivity. Assume negativity is at time zero; as time moves on, change should be guided to positivity. The time variable is influenced by rest, speeding up with grandiose thinking, slowing down for depression, or fortified by the word or unyielding sheer will. This should assess whether the user is still in the void or out of it. A further image: a “key term of mind structure to make radiation stop with stopping power.”

**Relations.** Consistent with P1 (peace from grace, not from elapsed time or from will-as-payment), P17 (waiting is not laziness), P22 (rest can feel like the void, so rest does not automatically speed recovery), P23 (given identity, not proving), P26 (a stall is needed; time alone does not interrupt a spiral), P27–P31 (connection, memory, word as two readings), and the earlier finding that Fighting Through costs capacity. **Dangerous readings:** that the clock healing the person is enough; that unyielding will is the way out; that a physics formula will stop rumination.

**Research.** *Supported as a cousin for felt time slowing in depression; untested as a law that time heals.* Thönes and Oberfeld (2015) meta-analyzed 16 studies (433 depressed participants, 485 controls). Depression had a medium effect on the subjective flow of time (Hedges’ g = 0.66): time felt as if it were passing more slowly. Duration judgments (estimation, production, reproduction, discrimination) were not significantly affected. So a slower *felt* clock is a fair labeled term; a change in objective timing accuracy is not what the source shows. Recovery after effort supports rest as a helper when it is actually rest (Meijman & Mulder, 1998; Sonnentag & Fritz, 2007). Rest-as-void (P22) limits that: for a high-I person, rest without a record raised threat. Rumination as brooding, not reflection, is the component of the Ruminative Response Scale more clearly tied to later depression (Treynor, Gonzalez, & Nolen-Hoeksema, 2003). Social connection’s health relevance is already cited (Holt-Lunstad, Smith, & Layton, 2010). Buffering by support is already cited (Cohen & Wills, 1985). Memory of kindness is already cited (Speer & Delgado, 2017). DBT skills are already cited (Linehan, 2015). *No verified mania-timing meta-analysis was attached this turn;* grandiose speeding of the clock is a labeled assumption (fast motion, not automatically progress). Radiation stopping power is a physics metaphor: linear stopping power is the energy a particle loses per unit path length. What maps onto something real is a *buffer* that reduces how much rumination reaches valence. What does not map is a “key term” that stops radiation in the mind.

**Toy result (a priori params, no feedback into the original ODEs; I = 0 recovered Unassisted load 0.286 / peak 0.481 / Y 33.38).** Starting x = −0.55. Unassisted finished at x = −1.00 and spent the whole run classified in the void: calendar time did not heal. Reset Flow mean x = 0.789, 44.8% of time out. Zero-Debt Grace mean x = 0.844, 47.6% out, and the highest felt-time rate (mean φ = 0.864, τ = 259 in 300 calendar units). Grace + Chosen Burst was nearly the same (mean x = 0.839, 47.2% out). Fighting Through ended at x = +0.874 but spent 70.9% of the run at the edge and only 10.9% out; mean x was only 0.126. High-I Fighting Through and Unassisted both finished at x = −1 (73% and 100% void). Raising the grandiose-speeding coefficient increased high-I Fighting’s φ (0.545 → 0.676) without moving x. Giving sheer will the same clock-steadying as “word” raised I = 0 Fighting’s final x (0.874 → 0.950) and φ a little, but high-I Fighting stayed at x = −1. The buffer (K_STOP) helped I = 0 Fighting’s final x a little (0.821 → 0.918) and did not rescue high-I Fighting. Combined (record + decoupling) spent 81.8% of time out; Grace + Record was close on valence (78.4% out). Rest-only could finish at x = +1 (mean 0.301) even while mean f_c stayed in the relapse range (0.152): valence is not the same as stopping compulsive fight.

**Reconciled wording.** “Negativity can be where a void begins, but time itself does not walk a person out. An effective clock speeds up with rest that is not erasure, feels slow when depression is heavy, and can race with grandiose fight without that race being progress. Scripture or an affirmation can steady the clock toward a livable rate; unyielding will is effort, not a guarantee. What moves a person toward positivity is grace, remembered kindness, connection, skill, and enough that closes—against rumination, isolation, and fusion. A buffer can absorb some of rumination’s pull; that image is a metaphor for protection, not radiation physics. Whether someone is in the void, at the edge, or out is a guide from a few short questions, not a diagnosis.”

Threat = A, control = P, bridge = y (value-to-action transfer) from the Gated Line and Void-State models.

**Relations.** Consistent with P4 / P5 / P25 (do not demand seeking or rebuilding when the gate is narrow; endure rather than lecture), with P6 (do not show a grade), with P25’s refusal to romanticize collapse (Ariel Words’ EndureBlocklist), with P26 and P30 (do not pep-talk or argue someone out of the void), and with P32 (a well-fitted story can act as a small “word” pulse on valence). **Tension with P6** if the composite score is shown to the person. **Tension with P25** if a high-a endure story preaches hitting bottom. **Tension with P26 / P30** if “sustain” is read as relentless positivity.

**Research.** *Untested* as matching original stories to an (A, P, Ỹ) triple. *Supported as cousins of graded demand, not of this score:* Csikszentmihalyi (1990) on challenge–skill balance in flow (activities, not narratives); Bower and Gilbody (2005) on stepped care as a research agenda (least-restrictive treatment still likely to help, then step up); Cuijpers, van Straten, and Warmerdam (2007), a meta-analysis of 16 behavioral-activation RCTs (N = 780) in which activity scheduling beat control at post-test (ES = 0.87, 95% CI 0.60–1.15) and did not differ significantly from cognitive therapy; Siegel (1999) on a window of tolerance, cited as a *concept*; Yerkes and Dodson (1908), already in the paper, as a caution against high demand at high arousal. *Supported as cousins of structured reading, not of fiction-matching:* Marrs (1995), a meta-analysis of 70 bibliotherapy samples, mean d = +0.565, no significant difference from therapist-administered treatments and no significant erosion at follow-up, stronger for assertion / anxiety / sexual dysfunction than for weight loss / impulse control / studying; Gualano et al. (2017), a *systematic review* (not a meta-analysis) of randomized trials on long-term bibliotherapy for depression—ten articles reporting eight studies, N = 1,347, follow-up from three months to three years; six adult studies reported a decrease in depressive symptoms, four youth studies did not show significant long-term results.

**Toy result (a priori params; policy = none recovers Unassisted load 0.286 / Y 33.38).** Twelve scheduled story times. Benefit +0.10 · fit on valence x; harm = +0.07 on A when b > Ỹ + m. Matched vs random (8 seeds) vs always-sustain vs none.

- Unassisted: matched mean x −0.757 (none −0.912), still 100% void, final x −0.975, 11 endure + 1 out, 0% overshoot. Always-sustain mean x −0.913, 100% overshoot, peak A 0.540 → 0.603.
- Zero-Debt Grace: matched mean x +0.852 (none +0.844), time out 48.1% vs 47.6%, 3 endure + 1 out + 8 sustain, 0% overshoot. Always-sustain matched none on mean x (+0.844), overshot every time, peak A 0.477 → 0.521, time out 47.5%.
- High-I Unassisted: matched mean x −0.975 (none −0.981), still 100% void, 12 endure, but 25% overshoot (3 of 12) because even b = 0.10 exceeded Ỹ + m when Ỹ fell to about 0.01–0.02; peak A 0.904 → 0.969. Always-sustain 100% overshoot, peak A 0.970.
- High-I Combined: matched mean x +0.827 (none +0.815), time out 82.4% vs 81.8%, 4 endure + 8 sustain, 0% overshoot. Always-sustain +0.814, slightly worse than silence.

Surprises kept: brief matched pulses lifted mean valence but did not walk Unassisted out of the void; relentless positivity never beat doing nothing; matching helped only a little once grace was already in place; at the deepest collapse the library’s gentlest story still asked too much, so a stricter gate would stay silent rather than tell the least-bad story.

**Reconciled wording.** “A story can be asked how well it meets the person’s threat, how much control it asks, and how large a next step it invites. Fit is closeness in that triple, with a hard stop when the invited action exceeds the effective bridge—the gate width times value-to-action transfer—plus a small margin: never ask more than the gate allows. Word-match still counts, but it does not override that gate. The resulting score grades the library, not the person, and is never shown. Endure, out, and sustain are regions of the same space: stay present and name the threat when the gate is narrow; offer one doable step in the recovering middle; offer a stay-out story when threat is low, control is high, and the gate is wide. Matching stories this way has not been tested in people. Relentless positivity is not the stay-out story.”
