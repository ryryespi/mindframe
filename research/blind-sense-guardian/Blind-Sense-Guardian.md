## Abstract

Ryan Espinosa proposes that people should be steady in their mental health before they enter simulated worlds, and that technology should enhance the senses and offer chosen environments, including ones a person can escape into, while leaving the person in charge of every connection. His central and most original claim is a **Sensory Separation postulate**. When a simulation couples several senses at once, and especially when it couples senses that are never coupled in ordinary life, the mind treats the combination as real. Leaving becomes harder, and in isolation a person may drift toward what he calls a "mania of non-humanity." He therefore proposes that **one sense should always be left "blind"** to the simulation, and that **guardians** (a visible light saying "you are not alone," an exit cue that tells the brain it has left, and techniques to snap out) should bring curious minds back to their people, culture, and lawful conduct. The fault should be placed on the environment, not the human.

This paper reads every readable file in Ryan's three Google Drive research folders (Alexa files, Grok files, Gemini files, and their subfolders: 45 distinct readable documents after removing duplicates) and maps each one onto the eight parts of the postulate. The files converge strongly on six parts of it:

1. Readiness before demand.
2. Human authorship of the environment's rules.
3. A living witness so no one enters the void alone.
4. A pre-set exit cue backed by a body-based snap-out.
5. A structured re-entry to people.
6. Language that puts the fault on the environment rather than the person.

The files say almost nothing directly about **sense-coupling** or the **blind sense**. Those are genuinely new in this postulate. The published science on multisensory integration, presence, body-ownership illusions, and virtual-reality aftereffects partly supports them. Coupled, synchronous senses do strengthen the feeling that a virtual place and body are real (Slater 2009; Botvinick and Cohen 1998; Petkova and Ehrsson 2008). Immersive VR can produce mild, short-lived depersonalization and derealization (Aardema et al. 2010; Peckmann et al. 2022). The same science also pushes back in three places:

- Multisensory immersion is also what makes VR exposure therapy work (Carl et al. 2019).
- Removing senses altogether can itself produce perceptual disturbances (Mason and Brady 2009).
- People in VR usually still *know* they are not "there" (Slater 2009).

The paper therefore refines the postulate. Instead of banning coupled senses, it proposes a **sense-coupling budget** set by the user while calm. Instead of leaving a sense dark, it keeps a **"blind" sense anchored to the real room**, never deprived. The light in the dark becomes a **witness light that points to a living human**. That last change is required by Ryan's own Project Guardian V2, which holds that AI can amplify a witness but cannot be one. The paper ends with twelve design features, each mapped to its source files, five falsifiable predictions, a small three-arm study, and an honest list of the conflicts between the files and the postulate.

## 1. The postulate, in Ryan's words

Quoted from Ryan's message of 15 November 2026. The wording is lightly cleaned for punctuation and readability only, and nothing has been added to its meaning.

> Look for connections in all these researches about completing a user's mental health first, before the use of technology. Technology should focus on enhancing human senses and simulated environments, while the user is in charge of connections and is able to choose the environment they live in. Humans always fight to control their environment and want the ability to escape, and this should be addressed by AI or by new tools invented by humans: an environment to pursue happiness, the ability to feel satisfied, and to move to another reality.
>
> The problem with technology is that it simulates a combination of senses that is no different from a fantasy created by the human mind. In my opinion, and as my postulate, the new technology should guard the combination of senses in simulated environments, such as no sound with visual, or no movement with sound or visual, keeping them separate. Once a simulation triggers multiple senses at once, it will be harder to escape the environment, for the mind will treat a combination of senses as a reality. Other simulations will connect senses that would not be connected in real life, raising multiple unethical issues: simulated realities that, by curiosity, trigger parts of the brain not meant to be connected, producing mental issues. In isolation with media, the human will start to develop inhuman desires, no different from a mania of non-humanity, and the human will eventually blend with the AI or new technology to feed off narratives even when they are unethical, and no witness can stop the simulation or the triggering of senses that are also triggered in reality. There must be a factor in simulated experience where one sense is left blind, to keep the user from being trapped in fantasy.
>
> Find ways in all these researches to guard individuals and guide them as their curiosity takes them in and out of realities, so they can return to social norms and back to their culture or lawful conduct: a visible cue that they are not alone, like a light in the dark, until their fantasies and curiosity are satisfied; a visible or sensory escape that triggers the brain to know it is no longer in a simulated environment; and techniques to snap out of mania and see a world beyond technology. I am not saying technology should be limited and avoided, but we need digital or physical guardians to bring curious minds back to being ethical and to know that it was the environment's fault and not the human's.
>
> — Ryan Espinosa

## 4. The convergent mechanism: a seven-step Guardian arc

Read together, the files describe one arc that Ryan never wrote down in a single place. A person (1) **arrives ready**, (2) **chooses and authors** the environment, (3) **enters on a budget**, with one sense still anchored outside, (4) **is never alone** while inside, (5) **leaves on a cue** and has a body-based way to snap out, (6) **re-enters** people and culture through a short ritual, and (7) **closes** the session with rest and a statement of completion, with no blame. The steps below show where each one comes from. Each one claims only what its source files say.

### 4.1 Arrive ready (G1)

Every protective file in the set puts readiness before demand. Project Guardian's Mental Guardian keeps an Identity Anchor, a Priority Vector, a Void-State Protocol, a Drift Baseline, and a Re-witnessing Schedule, and treats drift as "data — not judgment." The Ariel Handoff (Postulate 7) says real learning "can't happen when the mental or artistic mind is in turmoil," and concludes, "assess only when calm." The Void-State papers forbid provoking "trauma recall, sleep loss, mania, severe emptiness, or collapse." The State Finder and the Resonance Weave give a one-tap, non-diagnostic readiness check, and the Weave rewards honesty: "Honest reporting of low energy or high stress is treated as a successful move."

So *complete mental health first* becomes, in practice, *a calm-enough state, freely reported, before immersion*. Ryan's files never demand that someone be "cured" before entering. Void-state-grace's finding that support should not be "a debt" argues against making readiness a test the user must pass. **Grade: Plausible** as a design rule, with adjacent support from the clinical exclusion criteria Ryan already wrote into Checkpoint v1.1 ("acute crisis, current mania or psychosis, and active prolonged grief disorder").

### 4.2 Choose and author the environment (G2, G3)

The Four-Box architecture separates powers: "Humans author the System Equations (normative values, telos, and safety limits), while AI manages the Translation Equations." ESC adds that "Human wellbeing, consent, and rest override all three components." The I-to-O rule ("whoever makes the first or the last move holds the layer") and the Ariel prompt (only the user writes the anchor layer) make the user the author of their world. The Thermodynamics of Devotion explains why coercion fails: "Forced obligation is a thermodynamic violation."

The wish to escape (G3) is honored rather than pathologized. Ariel p calls withdrawal "intentional Topological Faraday Cages," and the Existential Crisis paper calls the void "the space where the next meaning is waiting to be made." The Zero-Point framework adds a crucial nuance: "True rest (*Kairos*) cannot exist in a vacuum; it requires the preceding polarity of marketplace noise." Escape and return are one loop, not opposites.

### 4.3 Enter on a budget, with one sense outside (G4, G5, G6)

This is where Ryan's postulate goes beyond his files. The closest file statements are these:

- C-R-Z P2: "Fantasy both connects and traps," and the instruction that absorption be "allowed on purpose, with a span and an exit cue."
- Project Ariel's wordless Reanchoring UI, which deliberately uses one channel (visual geometry, no words) to avoid "domain collapse." That is a working example of sensory separation chosen for safety.
- ESC's claim that qualia "cannot be translated — only pointed to, and shared in person." If the felt quality of experience is the part that never maps, then a design that keeps one channel tied to the real world keeps one piece of the person's own ground in the loop.
- Coherence Under Decay: "No system reaches perfect isolation or perfect zero." A sealed simulation is impossible in principle, so the honest move is to *choose* which channel stays open.
- The System Architecture Specification's "Environmental Primacy," under which outer reality outranks the inner layer.

BSF contributes a vocabulary, not evidence. Its stiffness coefficient η can name **how firm the boundary between simulated and real channels is set**: high η for a person who is tired or isolated, lower η for a rested person in a therapy session with a clinician.

What the files do *not* contain is any claim about which sense pairs are dangerous, or any data on sense-coupling. That part is Ryan's alone, and Section 5 tests it against the published literature.

### 4.4 Never alone inside: the witness light (G7a)

Here the files speak with one voice. Project Guardian: "ensure no one enters it alone." The Existential Crisis paper: "The witness must be human, with something to lose." Ariel connects students to "kindred minds ... so they know they are not alone." After the Line names the light directly: "Keeping a light lit and carrying its light into another room are different tasks." In Chapter 24 of the novel, a seawall community brings forty-one people home through a flood "in the dark" with one rule, "Call what you see. Read back what you hear. Here. Heard."

**The design consequence is firm.** Project Guardian V2 holds that AI fails all three of its witness criteria ("Nothing to Lose," "No Costly Action Confirmation," "No Continuous Accountability Over Time") and all three of its adversarial stress tests, and that this failure is "a structural safeguard." So the *light in the dark* may be digital in its form, as an indicator in the headset, but it must be a **witness light**: a sign that *a named living person* is reachable and has agreed to answer. "AI surfaces the moment. A human pays the cost."

### 4.5 Leave on a cue, and snap out (G7b, G7c)

The files offer a layered exit:

- **Planned absorption with an exit cue** (C-R-Z §5): "Decide, in advance, what will end it — a timer, a bell, a doorway, a phrase, a living voice."
- **The limit of that plan** (Checkpoint v1.1, C7): "absorbed states can erase the very meta-awareness the exit needs." The cue must therefore be *external and automatic*, not something the absorbed person has to remember to trigger.
- **The Four-Phase Exit Protocol** (Resonance Weave): the discord resolves, a closing reflection, "a gentle invitation to close the screen and spend time with real people," and then "The Blank Screen."
- **Clean egress** (Coherence Under Decay): "Single-tap exit, state saved, no 'you'll lose X' messages."
- **The 4-Pillar Grounding Protocol** (Void-State & Ariel): Pause ("close the screens, and step away"), Physical Grounding ("touch solid objects ... step outside"), Zero-Debt Grace, and Real Connection.
- **The snap-out for high-speed or grandiose states** (Sovereign Velocity): the Neutral-Axis Reset with a "3-point ambient visual sweep" and the "Grandiosity Brake," which uses humor at one's own machinery to return "humbly to flat-footed earth." The Morning Calls song carries the same kit: "I name five things the light is touching ... My hands are here, my feet are on the floor."
- **A recognition rule** (Ruminating Isolation): a loop counted as "3 recurrences / 10 min" triggers sensory grounding, rest, and social connection.

### 4.6 Re-enter people and culture (G7, return to norms)

Two files give unusually concrete guidance for the hardest part of G7, getting back to "social norms ... culture or lawful conduct." The Ruminating Isolation synthesis lists "Micro-Dosing Peripheral Contact," "Parallel Play," "Strict Time-Box Dosing," and "Transparency Without Performance." Checkpoint v1.3 adds the three-beat ritual: **Park it** ("one word or a note where you are"), **Turn** ("face the person: 'Here. Heard.'"), and **Return**. At institutional scale, Guardian V2 brings the Dunbar limit and the <25-person "witness cohorts" for a school of 700. Return is to *someone* in a group small enough to notice. The Tali sketch gives the cultural reason: "verbs of giving require a receiver."

### 4.7 Close, rest, and place the fault on the environment (G7a "until satisfied", G8)

Ryan's postulate says the light should stay on "until their fantasies and curiosity are satisfied." The files supply the satisfaction signal:

- Zero-Point's "Saturation (Epistemic Completion)."
- The Resonance Weave's exit after skills are mastered.
- Ariel's "Enough" state ("Nothing more to prove").
- Box 4's "statements of completion," and the simulation's own closing line, "Nothing left to seek this session. Done. Here."

Being satisfied counts as a real outcome here. Nobody has to be forced out.

On fault, Ryan's earlier work is unambiguous. The "never-fails" role "must not imply blame when symptoms persist." Guardian: discrepancy is "data — not judgment." The Weave: "A tired tank is asking for fuel, not passing a verdict." Durkheim's anomie, cited in both Guardian papers, places the cause of much existential collapse in institutions that "fail to provide meaning, belonging, or accountability." The files also keep one boundary. ESC and Guardian still ask for *costly action* on the way back, freely chosen, as evidence and never as a target. So the synthesis rule is: **the environment carries the fault, and the human keeps the return.**

## 5. Grounding in published science

The aim is to test each mechanism in Section 4 against verified literature, including where that literature cuts against the postulate.

| Claim in the postulate | What verified research shows | Grade |
|---|---|---|
| **G4/G5a.** Coupled senses make a simulation feel real. | Slater (2009) separates *place illusion* ("being there"), which is "constrained by the sensorimotor contingencies afforded by the virtual reality system," from *plausibility illusion*, and argues that "when both PI and Psi occur, participants will respond realistically." Synchronous seeing and feeling produce ownership of a rubber hand (Botvinick and Cohen 1998) and even of another body (Petkova and Ehrsson 2008). In an early VR study, adding tactile, olfactory, and auditory cues increased presence and memory for the environment, while adding visual detail did not (Dinh et al. 1999). That result fits the postulate: the *number of coupled senses* mattered more than the fidelity of any one sense. | **Supported** that coupled, synchronous senses raise felt realness and realistic responding. |
| **G5b.** "The mind will treat a combination of senses as a reality." | Partly true. Bodies and behavior respond as if real, but in Slater's account "the participant knows for sure that they are not 'there.'" Knowledge and response come apart. | **Plausible**, with this correction: the *response* is captured more than the *belief*. |
| **G5c.** Simulations can connect senses that are not connected in real life, producing experiences that are not in the world. | Multisensory integration is a basic brain operation (Stein and Stanford 2008), and the brain weights senses by reliability (Ernst and Banks 2002). Mismatched pairings create new percepts: a seen mouth changes a heard syllable (McGurk and MacDonald 1976), and heard beeps create illusory flashes (Shams, Kamitani, and Shimojo 2000). Conflicting visual and vestibular cues are a classic explanation of cybersickness (LaViola 2000; measured with the Simulator Sickness Questionnaire, Kennedy et al. 1993). | **Supported** that unnatural couplings produce real, unintended perceptual and bodily effects. **Speculative** that they "trigger parts of the brain not meant to be connected" in a lasting or harmful way. |
| **G5d.** Coupled immersion makes leaving harder and can loosen one's grip on ordinary reality. | After a VR session, a nonclinical sample showed increased depersonalization and derealization and "a lessened sense of presence in objective reality." Higher prior dissociation and a tendency to become absorbed predicted larger increases (Aardema et al. 2010). A randomized trial found more DP/DR symptoms right after VR gaming than after PC gaming. The symptoms were mild, and group differences were not significant at later follow-ups. VR also raised the perceived realness of the game world (Peckmann et al. 2022). | **Supported** for *mild, transient* aftereffects that are larger in absorption-prone people. **Not supported** for lasting harm in the general population on current evidence. |
| **G5e.** "In isolation with media ... inhuman desires," a "mania of non-humanity." | No study measures "inhuman desires." Nearby findings: absorbing fantasy can become *maladaptive daydreaming* when it replaces ordinary functioning (Somer 2002). Narrative transportation can quiet counterarguing (Green and Brock 2000). Population links between adolescent well-being and digital technology use are small (Orben and Przybylski 2019), and moderate use is not linked to harm (Przybylski and Weinstein 2017). Ryan's files themselves treat mania as a clinical condition that "is not simply high SNS activity" (Void-State Framework). | **Speculative** as stated. It is best kept as Ryan's warning and values claim, and studied through measurable proxies (displacement of sleep, work, and relationships; absorption; DP/DR) rather than as a diagnosis. |
| **G6.** Leave one sense "blind." | No direct test was found. The deprivation literature warns *against* reading "blind" as "dark": brief isolation from sound and vision produced perceptual disturbances, paranoia, and anhedonia, more so in hallucination-prone people (Mason and Brady 2009). Ryan's Ruminating Isolation file says the same ("hallucinatory compensation"). | **Speculative**, and refined in this paper: the blind sense should be **left un-simulated and connected to the real room**, not deprived. |
| **G7a.** A visible cue that you are not alone. | Strong social relationships predict survival (Holt-Lunstad, Smith, and Layton 2010, as cited in Guardian V2). Conversational read-backs establish shared understanding (Clark and Brennan 1991, as verified in Ryan's Checkpoint v1.1). No study tests a *witness light* in VR. | **Supported** for the value of a living tie; **Plausible** for this specific cue. |
| **G7b.** A pre-set exit cue. | If-then plans have reliable effects (Gollwitzer 1999), but absorption can dissociate experience from meta-awareness (Schooler 2002), so a self-triggered exit may fail. Reality monitoring, the act of telling perceived from imagined experience, is a known and fallible process (Johnson and Raye 1981; Johnson, Hashtroudi, and Lindsay 1993). | **Plausible**, provided the cue is external and automatic. |
| **G8.** Not anti-technology. | VR exposure therapy for anxiety disorders outperforms waitlist controls and is comparable to in-vivo exposure (Powers and Emmelkamp 2008; Carl et al. 2019). VR is a growing tool for assessment and treatment in mental health (Freeman et al. 2017). | **Supported.** It is also the strongest reason the separation rule must be a *budget*, not a ban: the multisensory realism that worries Ryan is the same thing that makes exposure therapy work. |

**Measures that already exist for testing the postulate.** Presence: Witmer and Singer (1998). State depersonalization: the Cambridge Depersonalisation Scale (Sierra and Berrios 2000), which Ryan's Void-State and Gated Line papers already list. Cybersickness: Kennedy et al. (1993).

## 7. Testable predictions and a small study

### 7.1 Predictions (each can fail)

1. **Blind sense lowers aftereffects.** Compared with a fully coupled session, a session with one channel left real (F4) will produce a smaller pre-to-post increase in state depersonalization (CDS-State).
2. **Blind sense costs little enjoyment.** Enjoyment and satisfaction in the blind-sense session will be non-inferior to full coupling within a pre-set margin, even if presence is lower.
3. **The witness light speeds return.** Adding F5 and F8 will shorten time-to-reorientation after exit (for example, latency to answer a real-room probe question correctly) and reduce unplanned overruns.
4. **Absorption-prone people benefit most.** Following Aardema et al. (2010), the protective effect of predictions 1 and 3 will be larger in participants with higher baseline absorption or dissociation.
5. **The strong form can fail.** If blind-sense sessions show no DP/DR advantage *and* clearly lower enjoyment, the Sensory Separation postulate in its strong form is not supported for healthy adults. The budget (F3) would then rest on consent and preference alone.

### 7.2 Study sketch: "Blind Sense Pilot" (proposal only)

**Design.** A within-subjects, counterbalanced, three-condition laboratory study. All three conditions use the same 15-minute exploratory VR environment.

- **A, Full coupling:** synthetic visuals, synthetic spatial audio, and synchronized controller haptics.
- **B, Blind sense:** the same visuals and haptics, with *room audio passthrough* in place of synthetic audio. The real room stays audible.
- **C, Blind sense + Guardian:** condition B plus the witness light, an external exit bell, and the four-phase exit with Park it / Turn / Return to a waiting researcher.

**Participants.** 36–48 healthy adults. Exclude anyone with a history of psychosis, mania, dissociative disorder, seizures, current acute distress, or prolonged grief disorder, consistent with Ryan's Checkpoint v1.1 exclusions. Screen with a readiness check like F1.

**Measures.**

- CDS-State, before and after each session (Sierra and Berrios 2000).
- The Presence Questionnaire (Witmer and Singer 1998).
- The Simulator Sickness Questionnaire (Kennedy et al. 1993).
- Enjoyment and satisfaction (single items, pre-registered).
- Time-to-reorientation on a real-room probe.
- Unplanned overrun.
- Baseline absorption and dissociation (instrument chosen and verified at pre-registration).

**Safety and ethics.** Ethics or IRB review before recruitment, usually through a university lab partner. Written consent. A 988 safety flow. Stop rules for rising distress or emerging DP/DR beyond mild. A debrief that uses environment-first language. Synthetic-data analysis script and pre-registration on OSF before any data. Ryan, as PI, owns ethics, consent, and safety. AI assistants are not co-investigators, matching his Checkpoint v1.3 roles.

**Primary analysis.** A mixed model with condition and absorption as predictors and CDS-State change as the outcome; a non-inferiority test for enjoyment (B vs A); time-to-reorientation (C vs B).

## 8. Conflicts and tensions between the files and the postulate

1. **Digital guardians vs. AI cannot be a witness.** G8 welcomes "digital or physical guardians." Project Guardian V2 concludes that "AI remains permanently in the scaffold layer when governing human protection." *Resolution:* digital guardians are allowed as **lights, cues, and circuit breakers**, and every light must point to a living person. In AI-to-AI layers, Guardian V2 permits full automatic enforcement.
2. **Separation vs. multisensory healing.** VR exposure therapy works partly *because* it is multisensory and convincing. Across 30 randomized trials it was as effective as in-vivo exposure (Carl et al. 2019). A strict "no sound with visual" rule would also forbid film, music videos, and most games. *Resolution:* a user-set **coupling budget** with therapy-grade exceptions under a clinician, not a ban.
3. **Blind vs. deprived.** Ryan's Ruminating Isolation synthesis and Mason and Brady (2009) both show that removing senses can *produce* perceptual disturbance. *Resolution:* the blind sense is kept **real**, never switched off.
4. **User in charge vs. absorption erasing self-noticing.** G2 places the user in charge, but Checkpoint v1.1 (C7) records that absorption "can erase the very meta-awareness the exit needs." *Resolution:* the user decides **in advance, while calm** (F2, F6), and an external cue enforces what they chose. They remain in charge, through a promise made beforehand.
5. **Ryan's own Connection Postulate.** C-R-Z P5 asks for "connection that triggers all senses" in real conversation. This is consistent rather than contradictory: full sensory richness is reserved for **living reality**, and simulation gets fewer coupled senses. The asymmetry is itself a reality cue.
6. **Ariel's "never rebukes" vs. fantasy as a trap.** Ariel finds frames where a dream "can still hold," while G5 warns that fantasy traps. The Handoff leaves this open (Open thread 2: healthy acceptance vs. vanity). *Proposed resolution:* the guardian never rebukes the **person**, and it does limit the **environment** through F3, F6, and F10.
7. **Reward mechanics.** The Marketplace's lottery and currency are themselves environment designs that could feed compulsive checking. Ryan's Coherence Under Decay already flags "Live ticking counters invite compulsive checking" and prefers deterministic recognition.
8. **Over-strong claims inside the files.** Ariel p's "Fall of the Categorical DSM" and its precise-diagnosis language, the Ruminating Isolation pilot's implausible effect sizes (N = 1, d = −12.39), and the physics-to-mind mappings in BSF and Coherence Under Decay are not evidence about minds. Coherence Under Decay states this limit itself. They are used here only as metaphor or design inspiration.
9. **"Environment's fault" vs. accountability.** ESC and Guardian keep personal costly action and accountability. The synthesis holds both: *the environment carries the fault; the human keeps the return.*

## 9. Limitations

- **This is a synthesis of conceptual files, not of data.** Almost every file in the three folders is labeled by Ryan or his assistants as conceptual, a toy model, or a design. Agreement among them shows internal coherence, not empirical truth.
- **Several files were co-written with AI assistants** (Grok, Gemini, Alexa). Their convergence may partly reflect shared training or shared prompts. Ryan's Universal Translation report already names this "shared-training caveat."
- **Sense-coupling and the blind sense have no direct test in the literature found here.** The supporting science is about presence, illusions, and VR aftereffects in general, and mostly comes from short sessions with healthy adults.
- **Mental-health terms are used carefully.** Nothing here diagnoses anyone, and readiness checks are not clinical screens. "Mania of non-humanity" is Ryan's phrase for a feared trajectory, and this paper does not treat it as a clinical entity.
- **Faith commitments** (grace, "with" rather than "for," Christ as the "Universal Angle") appear in many files and in Ryan's dedication. They are recorded as **[Values]**: honored, and not tested.
- **Not every file was read in full.** Some items were read only in part or not at all (audio, a music score, a large novel PDF covered by its in-bundle analysis, and a UI build). See Appendix A.

## 10. Closing

Ryan's postulate asks for something gentle and demanding at once: let people wander as far as their curiosity takes them, and make sure the way home is always lit. His research files had already built most of the road back:

- a readiness check that never shames,
- human authorship of the world's rules,
- a witness who answers "Heard,"
- an exit that does not punish,
- a body-first snap-out,
- a short ritual for turning back to people,
- and a rest that counts as an outcome.

What his new postulate adds is the piece the files were missing: **what the simulation itself should be allowed to do to the senses.** The answer this synthesis proposes is a coupling budget the user sets while calm, one sense always left to the real world, and a light that always means a real person. That answer is testable, and the study in Section 7 can show that it is wrong.

If it holds, the guardian it describes does not keep people out of other worlds. It makes those worlds leaveable, and it puts the fault on the environment. The way back stays with the person who wandered.
