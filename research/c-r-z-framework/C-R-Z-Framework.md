## Abstract

This note records Ryan Espinosa's **C-R-Z Framework** as developed in three prior working papers: *The Unified C-R-Z Somatic-Kinetic Framework*; *Returning to Reality and Neutralizing Threat Responses* (V1 and V2); and *Everyday C-R-Z: The Low-Friction Architecture*. The model is meant to bypass slow verbal introspection, neutralize threat responses, and preserve personal sovereignty through three pillars — **Calibration (c)**, **Reality-Return (r)**, and **Zero-Velocity (z)** — and a brief action protocol, **Drop-Press-Snap**, with later daily adaptations.

The first task is to present that architecture faithfully. The second is to grade each major claim against literature actually checked for this draft, using three evidence tiers: **supported**, **plausible**, and **speculative**. The third is to cross-reference the framework with two related notes in this collection, *Coherence Under Decay* and the quantum boundary proposal, and with the Orun story's skill of dropping everything, listening for a key to reality, and exiting the gate. A later section, Attention as Selu, asks how a person may visit an inner or fictional world without letting it override the living one, and treats attention as a limited coin, like Orun's selu, spent between those worlds. A further section records the author's Connection Postulate: purpose keeps turning in the mind until connection with living people can complete it; fantasy both connects and traps; reality must be checked against shame, guilt, and false gossip; connection takes priority, including a respectful drop toward God and toward people who reach out; and the hope, held as a values claim, is that when memories fade, love remains, and that being present to respond beats seeking more knowledge. The note then proposes a small, testable within-subject pilot of Drop-Press-Snap against a control, on subjective distress and heart rate, and closes with safety limits, including the need for clinical follow-up when mania warning signs appear, and when immersive daydreaming crowds out life.

The short version of the audit is this. Outward sensory reorienting, affect labeling, plantar contribution to posture, and slow, exhale-emphasized breathing have real literatures behind them. A brief motor task can interrupt rumination, but not specifically because an ASL "C" handshape bypasses the phonological loop. Shifting weight onto the soles is a reasonable proprioceptive anchor; a "solar plexus reflex point" on the foot is not a supported physiological mechanism. Expansive-posture hormone claims failed to replicate, though modest effects on how a posture *feels* are still discussed. "Glimmers," in Deb Dana's polyvagal-informed usage, are small cues of safety and must not be confused with mania prodromes. Polyvagal theory itself is disputed. C-R-Z agrees with *Coherence Under Decay* and with the Orun story on returning to a rest that is not empty and on leaving a loop. It conflicts with them if Zero-Velocity is read as a literal arrival at *v* = 0, and it conflicts with itself if "mania glimmers" and safety glimmers keep the same name.

## 0. Reader's guide and evidence tiers

C-R-Z is the author's framework. This note does not replace the three source papers. It is a companion audit: keep the model, credit the people whose work it touches, and say plainly where a postulate is ahead of the evidence.

Each claim below is tagged as one of:

- **Supported.** A real, identified literature backs the narrow claim. Support is never a claim that C-R-Z as a whole has been tested.
- **Plausible.** The claim is a reasonable extension of nearby findings, but the specific C-R-Z move, label, or bundle has not been shown to work as described.
- **Speculative.** The claim goes beyond current evidence, rests on a weak or disconfirmed tradition, or is best read as metaphor or a values statement.

A fourth label, **[Values]**, is used where the claim states what is worth protecting (sovereignty, non-extraction, grace, the dignity of leaving). Values are not tested by experiment and are not diminished by being labelled.

Sources in Section 12 were checked on 13 November 2026 against publisher, PubMed, PMC, or other bibliographic records and against abstracts or open full text returned in those records. They were not all read in their entirety. Page numbers and DOIs are given where the record supplied them.

## 2. The framework, presented faithfully

### 2.1 The three pillars

**Calibration (c).** Instantaneous mental and somatic tuning that uses minimal alphanumeric notation or ASL-mediated motor cues, such as an ASL "C" handshape, in order to bypass phonological-loop overhead and halt internal rumination.

**Reality-Return (r).** Immediate re-engagement with external sensory input, labeling of the environment, and attentional reorienting, in order to pull awareness out of internal threat simulations.

**Zero-Velocity (z).** Gravitational grounding, written *v* → 0, through plantar mechanoreceptor stimulation. The aim is to establish boundary sovereignty, halt fight-or-flight kinetic momentum, and prevent "negative perpetual motion."

The three letters are meant to be used together, not as a menu of optional moods. Calibration marks and interrupts the inner loop. Reality-Return aims attention at what can be verified outside the body of the thought. Zero-Velocity stills the body's forward threat-momentum by putting weight into contact with the ground.

### 2.2 The Drop-Press-Snap protocol

For acute threat or conflict, the source papers specify a split-second sequence:

1. **The Drop.** Instantly drop the shoulders and shift all body weight downward into the heels and soles against the earth.
2. **The Press.** Subtly press the big toe downward and apply pressure to the "solar plexus reflex point" on the sole, with the stated aim of triggering vagal down-regulation.
3. **The Snap.** Execute a micro-sign against the leg (the ASL "C" handshape) while anchoring attention externally to reality.

The protocol is intended to be brief enough to use in the middle of a conversation or a surge of alarm, without first narrating the feeling at length.

### 2.3 V2 and daily adaptations

The V2 expansion and the everyday, low-friction architecture add:

- **Breath mechanics:** inhaling "internal friction" and exhaling "collective peace."
- **The Unclenched Hand:** an uncrossed, open-hand posture.
- **A soft triangle gaze** with a "transparent glass-pane filter."
- **Early-warning detection** that tells triggers apart from "mania glimmers."

These are daily or preparatory habits, not only crisis moves. They are meant to lower the friction of using C-R-Z so that the protocol does not itself become another rumination.

### 3.2 Reality-Return: grounding, orienting, and labeling

Reality-Return is the pillar with the closest neighbors in existing care. Trauma-informed and dialectical-behavior-therapy (DBT) practice often asks a person in rising distress or dissociation to name what can be seen, touched, or heard in the room, so that attention leaves an inner replay and returns to a shared present. A 2025 paper that set out to build an operational definition of therapeutic grounding describes the shared core as engagement with basic sensory input, and sometimes with simple cognitive recall, in order to shift focus away from distressing internal stimuli (Hammond and Brown 2025). In DBT-PTSD programs, antidissociative and sensory-grounding skills are used so that exposure work can proceed; one feasibility study of a computer-assisted aid found that patients often rated such skills as useful for blocking dissociation (Görg et al. 2016). That is practice-level support for the *family* of methods, not a trial of C-R-Z.

The popular 5-4-3-2-1 countdown (five things seen, four heard, and so on) is a convenient script inside that family. No dedicated randomized trial of that exact sequence was identified for this draft. It should be cited as a widely taught clinical tool, not as a proven protocol.

Environmental labeling also touches a more specific finding. Lieberman, Eisenberger, Crockett, Tom, Pfeifer, and Way (2007) reported that putting feelings into words — affect labeling — reduced amygdala and other limbic responses to negative images, with increased right ventrolateral prefrontal activity, relative to other forms of encoding. C-R-Z's labeling is slightly different: it labels the *room* more than the *feeling*. The overlap is still real. Naming a chair, a window, or "this is a thought, that is the river" is a low-demand act of putting experience into words and pointing at something present. That is **supported** as a nearby mechanism, and **plausible** as the reason Reality-Return may work, pending a direct test.

Two cautions belong next to this support. First, for some people with trauma histories, turning attention *inward* onto bodily sensation can briefly raise arousal. Reality-Return is already written as an outward move, and it should stay that way. Second, grounding is a coping skill, not a treatment for PTSD, panic, or dissociation.

### 3.3 Calibration: the phonological loop, rumination, and the ASL "C"

Calibration rests on a standard model and on a much more specific hypothesis.

The standard model is **supported**. Baddeley and Hitch (1974) described working memory as a limited workspace with a verbal rehearsal loop. In later statements of the model, the phonological loop has a short-lived store and an articulatory rehearsal process; saying an irrelevant word out loud or silently ("articulatory suppression") impairs verbal short-term recall (Baddeley, Lewis, and Vallar 1984; reviewed in Baddeley 2012). If a stretch of rumination is carried as inner speech, then occupying the rehearsal process could, in principle, make that speech harder to keep going.

The specific hypothesis — that a minimal alphanumeric cue or an ASL "C" pressed against the leg *bypasses phonological-loop overhead and thereby halts rumination* — is **speculative**. Articulatory suppression, in the laboratory, is speech-like: repeating "the," mouthing, or otherwise engaging the articulators. A handshape against the thigh is a tactile and manual act. It may occupy attention, mark a decision, or interrupt a chain of thought. It is not, on present evidence, a way of taking the phonological loop offline.

A direct test of the nearby idea is already more modest than the C-R-Z wording. Nalborczyk, Perrone-Bertolotti, Baeyens, Grandchamp, Spinelli, Koster, and Lœvenbruck (2022) induced rumination in 106 adults and then had them spend five minutes on silent mouthing or on finger tapping. Self-reported rumination fell after *both* tasks. The advantage for articulatory suppression over tapping was only slight and uncertain. If that pattern holds, Calibration may work because a brief, deliberate motor act interrupts the loop, not because the ASL "C" is a privileged linguistic key. That is still useful. It just recommends humility about the handshape, and it licenses a seated or alternative cue for people who cannot or do not use that sign.

Minimal alphanumeric notation, used as a private shorthand instead of a long inner monologue, is likewise **plausible** as a way to reduce verbal load, and **speculative** as a complete bypass of introspection. Implementation intentions — a short, pre-decided "if-then" cue — have their own literature (Gollwitzer 1999). C-R-Z can borrow that structure without claiming a new memory-system effect.

### 3.4 Zero-Velocity: plantar input, the reflexology problem, and *v* → 0

Two claims need to be separated.

The first is **supported**. The sole of the foot is densely equipped with cutaneous mechanoreceptors. Kavounoudias, Roll, and Roll (1998) argued that the plantar sole behaves as a "dynamometric map" for human balance: stimulating different regions of the sole produces direction-specific postural responses. They later showed that foot-sole and ankle-muscle inputs contribute jointly to erect posture (Kavounoudias, Roll, and Roll 2001). A later review of single-unit recordings describes the innervation of the human foot sole in detail (Strzalkowski, Peters, Inglis, and Bent 2018). Shifting weight into the heels and soles, and pressing the big toe, will change that afferent pattern. As a way of "landing" in the present, this is **plausible** and physically real.

The second claim is **speculative** and should be withdrawn as a mechanism. The source papers ask the person to press a "solar plexus reflex point" on the sole in order to trigger vagal down-regulation. That wording comes from reflexology, which maps patches of the foot onto distant organs. Ernst, Posadzki, and Lee (2011), updating an earlier systematic review of randomized trials, concluded that the evidence does not demonstrate that reflexology is an effective treatment, and the tradition's organ maps remain unvalidated assumptions (see also Ernst 2009). There is no identified physiological pathway by which a named point on the sole would selectively regulate the solar plexus or the vagus in the manner claimed.

**Reframe, which the author requested in spirit by asking that postulates be cross-checked before they enter the papers:** keep the toe and sole press; drop the organ-specific point and the guaranteed vagal claim. Describe the press as a proprioceptive and tactile attention anchor — a way of feeling the ground on purpose. If vagal or heart-rate effects appear in a pilot, report them as outcomes, not as already-known results of a reflex point.

The notation *v* → 0 should be treated the same way. As a picture of coming to rest, it is clear. As physics or as a claim that fight-or-flight "kinetic momentum" is halted and "negative perpetual motion" is prevented, it is **speculative**. Bodies do not reach zero motion while alive and standing. *Coherence Under Decay* already insists that no real system reaches perfect isolation or perfect zero, and that a rest state should be reachable, honest, and never empty. Zero-Velocity is at its best when it means *approach rest; put weight into the ground; do not keep accelerating the inner chase*.

Dropping the shoulders can be kept as a simple release of upper-body bracing. It does not need a larger posture theory under it.

### 3.5 Posture: the Unclenched Hand, and what did not survive replication

V2's Unclenched Hand — uncrossed posture, open hands — should be stated modestly.

Carney, Cuddy, and Yap (2010) reported that brief "power poses" changed felt power, risk-taking, testosterone, and cortisol. Ranehill, Dreber, Johannesson, Leiberg, Sul, and Weber (2015), in a larger, experimenter-blind replication (*N* = 200), reproduced an effect on self-reported feelings of power but found no effect on hormones or risk-taking. Simmons and Simonsohn (2017) argued, with a *p*-curve analysis, that the evidential value of the broader power-posing literature was weak. Later replies and meta-analyses have been more favorable to effects on *felt power* and some affective ratings than to the original endocrine and behavioral package (Gronau et al. 2017; Cuddy, Schultz, and Fosse 2018; Elkjær, Mikkelsen, Michalak, Mennin, and O'Toole 2022). The honest summary is: **do not rest C-R-Z on power-posing physiology**. An open, uncrossed stance may still be a decent, low-cost cue of not bracing for a fight. That is **plausible** as comfort and self-signal, **speculative** as a unique regulator of threat circuitry.

A separate posture literature is slightly more useful here. Nair, Sagar, Sollers, Consedine, and Broadbent (2015) found that upright versus slumped seated posture affected mood, self-esteem, and responses during a stress task. That is not the Unclenched Hand, and it is not a license to overclaim, but it supports the general idea that how one sits or stands can color how a stressful moment feels.

### 3.6 Breath: what is supported, and what is a prayer in the breath

Slow breathing, often near six breaths per minute, has been associated with higher heart-rate variability and with reports of greater relaxation and lower anxiety in healthy adults, though the primary studies are often small and unevenly reported (Zaccaro, Piarulli, Laurino, Garbella, Menicucci, Neri, and Gemignani 2018). Balban, Neri, Kogon, Weed, Nouriani, Jo, Holl, Zeitzer, Spiegel, and Huberman (2023), in a remote randomized study (NCT05304000), compared five minutes a day of cyclic sighing (exhale-emphasized), box breathing, or cyclic hyperventilation with retention against mindfulness meditation, over one month. Breathwork, especially cyclic sighing, improved positive affect and reduced respiratory rate more than meditation. Resting heart rate and heart-rate variability did not change significantly over the month. That last point matters for any pilot that uses heart rate as a primary outcome: mood and respiratory rate may move when HR and HRV do not.

C-R-Z's own wording — inhale internal friction, exhale collective peace — is **[Values]** language riding on a **supported** class of practices. The phrases are a way of aiming the breath. They should not be presented as the mechanism. If a daily adaptation is written for others, pair the author's phrases with a simple, testable instruction (for example, a slightly longer exhale than inhale, for a few minutes), and let the phrases remain what they are: an intention, not a result.

### 3.7 Soft triangle gaze and the glass-pane filter

No trial of a "soft triangle gaze" with a "transparent glass-pane filter" was identified. The move can be read, charitably, as: rest the eyes on three outward points, keep the gaze soft, and imagine a little distance, as if looking through glass, so that attention stays in the room without staring or scanning for threat. As a form of outward attentional deployment, that is **plausible**. As a named, distinctive technique with special effects, it is **speculative**. In an everyday architecture it can stay, described in ordinary words, and should be dropped from any list of established mechanisms until someone tests it.

### 3.8 Early warning: triggers, glimmers, and mania

This is the place where careful naming is part of safety.

**Triggers**, in ordinary clinical speech, are cues that reopen threat, memory, or craving. Learning to notice them early is a standard aim of many therapies and of relapse-prevention work.

**Glimmers**, in Deb Dana's polyvagal-informed writing, are small, ordinary cues of safety and connection — a patch of light, a kind voice, a moment of ease — that can be noticed on purpose (Dana 2020). As a practice of attending to what is already bearable, this is **plausible** and compatible with Reality-Return. The theoretical wrapping is weaker. Polyvagal theory, as advanced by Porges (for example 2007), has been sharply criticized on physiological and comparative grounds. Grossman (2023) argues that several of its basic premises, including the reading of respiratory sinus arrhythmia as a straightforward index of a distinct "smart vagus," do not hold. C-R-Z can keep the habit of noticing small, real, present cues of safety without committing itself to a disputed autonomic architecture. Say that plainly.

**Mania early-warning signs** are a different object, and here the literature is **supported**. People with bipolar disorder often have a personal prodrome — reduced sleep, more talk, rising energy, irritability, racing thoughts, more spending, or more goal-directed activity — days to weeks before a manic or hypomanic episode (reviewed by various clinical groups; see also Perry, Tarrier, Morriss, McCarthy, and Limb 1999). A Cochrane review found that interventions to help people recognize early signs of recurrence can delay bipolar relapse (Morriss, Faizal, Jones, Williamson, Bolton, and McCarthy 2007). That work should be credited, used, and not renamed lightly.

The source papers' phrase **"mania glimmers"** is a conflict of terms. Dana's glimmers are cues of safety. Manic prodromes are cues that a person may be leaving ordinary range and may need a clinical plan, sleep protection, medication review, or contact with a clinician. Treating a manic early-warning sign as a glimmer risks exactly the misreading the V2 adaptation is trying to prevent: taking a rising, glittering, high-energy state for a sign that one is "coming back to life." The recommended reframe is:

- keep **glimmers** for small, steadying cues of present safety, or drop the word if it confuses;
- keep **early-warning signs** or **prodromes** for mania and hypomania;
- teach, where relevant, that the two can be told apart in part by sleep, duration, impairment, and whether other people are worried;
- treat mania warning signs as a reason for clinical follow-up, not as a prompt to run Drop-Press-Snap harder.

## 4. Cross-references

### 4.1 Inside C-R-Z

The three pillars hang together if they are read as one move: interrupt the inner monologue, point attention at the present, and put the body into contact with the ground. They pull apart if each pillar is asked to do unique physiological work that the others do not. Calibration and the Snap overlap (both are brief motor cues). Reality-Return and the last beat of the Snap overlap (both aim attention outward). Zero-Velocity and the Drop overlap (both put weight into the ground). That overlap is a strength for a low-friction practice — one short sequence, not three therapies — and a warning against claiming three independent mechanisms.

There is a deeper internal tension between "bypass verbal introspection" and Reality-Return's use of labeling. Lieberman's finding is that words can help. C-R-Z can resolve this by distinguishing *rumination* (repetitive, closed, self-referential speech) from *labeling* (short, outward, present-tense naming). The framework already wants fewer words, not no contact with language.

A second tension is between absolute sovereignty and the everyday architecture's interest in "collective peace." Sovereignty, in the better reading, is the right to stop, to refuse, and to leave. It is not a requirement to regulate alone. The Orun story, discussed below, is explicit that a living voice can snap a person back. That is a friend of Reality-Return, not a threat to it.

### 4.2 *Coherence Under Decay* and the quantum boundary proposal

*Coherence Under Decay* (Espinosa, with Grok Bot, 12 November 2026) joins the quantum boundary proposal to the Zero-Point chronomimetic notes under one control template: keep a system useful and coherent while decay, noise, and idle coupling pull on it; use bounded, gentle control; keep a protected rest state that is reachable, honest, and never empty. The quantum boundary proposal (same date) maps Ariel labels onto textbook qubit results and refuses to treat a metaphor as a gate voltage. Both notes are conceptual. They are not new physics.

**Where they agree with C-R-Z:**

- A return to rest is not a collapse into a void. Zero-Velocity, the Sanctuary in the economic notes, and Orun's teaching that rest is not emptiness are the same shape.
- Bounded, brief control is preferred to grinding effort. Drop-Press-Snap is a short intervention, not a campaign against the self. *Coherence Under Decay* likewise rejects fighting through as the only path and warns against compulsion mechanics.
- Honest naming matters. The quantum note refuses metaphorical overclaim; this audit should do the same for reflex points, *v* = 0, and polyvagal certainty.
- Clean egress is part of dignity. Reality-Return and "exit the gate" are kin to the app's single-tap exit and to the rule that rest must remain leaveable.
- Safety monitors should offer reflection and never punish. C-R-Z's early-warning idea belongs in that family, provided mania signs are routed to care rather than to a self-score.

**Where they conflict, or will conflict if wording is not tightened:**

- **Perfect zero.** C-R-Z's *v* → 0, read literally, collides with the physical and design lesson of *Coherence Under Decay*: no system reaches perfect isolation or perfect zero; a friction floor and a residual decay rate are features, not failures. The repair is already given in Section 3.4: Zero-Velocity is an approach to rest, not an arrival at nothing.
- **Mechanism versus template.** *Coherence Under Decay* is careful to call its bridge a structural analogy. C-R-Z sometimes speaks in "hardware-level" and vagal-guarantee language. The CUD standard should win: say what is analogy, what is design, and what would count as a measurement.
- **Isolation and sovereignty.** If Zero-Velocity's "boundary sovereignty" is taken to mean withdrawing from other people, it cuts against the Circuit of Grace and against the finding, in the author's own fiction, that relationships snap a person back. Sovereignty is better defined as not being overwritten, and as the right to leave, not as a closed system.

The quantum layer does not supply evidence for C-R-Z, and C-R-Z does not supply evidence for qubit control. Any shared vocabulary (*gate*, *zero*, *friction*) is analogical. That limit should stay visible.

### 4.3 The Orun story: drop everything, listen for a key, exit the gate

In *Selu Cannot Buy the Morning Back* and the game *The Path of Rivel*, Neru's knowledge-seeking and her imagined conversations with the dead can close into a loop. The skill the story finally asks for is to notice that loop, put down the purse and the maps, and listen for one real thing in the present — breath, the river, a living voice. That is the key that lets her exit the gate. The game gives this a meter: when the loop rises, a "Drop everything and listen" choice appears; it clears the loop, restores calm, and is required for the best ending. A later line in that work states the human version directly: relationships snap you back in reality.

**Agreement is close, and it is the best literary cross-check the framework has.**

- Calibration is the noticing: the loop is looping; stop adding turns.
- Reality-Return is the listening for a key that is *not* another inner conversation.
- Zero-Velocity is the putting-down: weight off the chase, into the ground, hands empty of the maps.
- Drop-Press-Snap is a bodily spelling of "drop everything and listen."
- V2's outward gaze and open hands match the story's refusal to keep bargaining with a closed inner room.

**The story also corrects the framework in two places.** First, the key is often a living other person, not only a plantar signal. A C-R-Z practice that cannot make room for a voice, a touch, or a shared commons is narrower than the author's own narrative. Second, the story will not let rest become a loophole, and it will not let knowledge bought from the dead replace consent and relationship. That is the same warning *Coherence Under Decay* gives the Sanctuary: a rest state that becomes a shelter for hoarding, rumination, or refusal of the living world is no longer rest.

There is no serious conflict of aim, only a conflict of emphasis if C-R-Z is practiced as a closed, solo circuit.

## 5. Attention as Selu: Switching Between Inner Worlds and Reality

The earlier sections treated C-R-Z as a way out of threat, rumination, and a closed loop with the dead. This section treats a kinder and more ordinary problem, which is also the author's: how to be lost in thought, or in a fictional world such as Orun, *without* letting that world override the living one, and how to come back when a real conversation is already underway.

In Orun, **selu** is the coin of the commons. It fades if it sits unused, and it cannot buy a life, a morning, or a person home. Here it is used as a picture of **attention**. Attention is limited. It can be spent on an inner world or on this room. Spent well on a living voice, it returns you to the commons. Hoarded inside a story, it thins what the people in front of you can reach. The author's line for this is short, and it is **[Values]** as well as design: **Attention is life.**

None of the amounts below are measured units. The budget is a metaphor, in the same family as the circulation and decay pictures in *Coherence Under Decay*. The techniques are graded, as before, against literature checked for this draft.

### 5.1 The honest science of "multitasking"

People often say they are multitasking when they are switching. For attention-demanding work, the brain does not run two full tasks in true parallel. It reconfigures, and the reconfiguration has a cost. Monsell (2003) reviewed the task-switching literature and described reliable switch costs in time and, often, in errors. Rubinstein, Meyer, and Evans (2001) found that alternation cost more when the rules were more complex, and less when a clear cue told the person which task was now in force. They modelled the work as goal shifting plus rule activation. That is **supported**. It is also the honest warning under this whole section: visiting Orun and listening to a friend are two tasks. Moving between them is a switch, not a free overlay. A cue that says "this is the living task now" is not a flourish. It is how switching gets cheaper.

Mind-wandering is common, and it is not free. Killingsworth and Gilbert (2010), sampling people through the day, found that minds were on something other than the current activity in nearly half of the samples, and that those moments were, on average, associated with less happiness than moments of attention to the present, including when the off-task thoughts were pleasant. That is **supported** as a large experience-sampling result. It is not a law that every daydream makes a person unhappy, and it is not a reason to crush imagination. It is a reason to notice when attention has left a life that still needs it.

Not all mind-wandering is the same. Seli, Risko, and Smilek (2016) showed that intentional and unintentional mind-wandering can move in opposite directions: an easy, boring task produced more *deliberate* wandering, a harder task more *unintended* drift, while the overall rate looked unchanged if the two were lumped together. Smallwood and Schooler (2015), reviewing the field, described a balance of costs (errors, decoupled perception) and possible benefits (planning, some forms of creativity). Deliberate, bounded wandering is therefore **plausible** as a useful state. Unintended wandering that the person does not notice is the costlier one.

The skill that makes a budget possible is **meta-awareness**: noticing that the mind has left. Schooler (2002) distinguished experience from the explicit awareness of that experience. People often fail to notice that they have drifted; unaware episodes tend to hurt performance more than ones the person has already caught (Smallwood and Schooler 2015). Catching the drift — "I am not in this conversation" — is **supported** as a real, trainable distinction, and it is the first move in the protocol below.

Two large-scale brain networks are often mentioned in this neighborhood. A default-mode network tends to be more active in undirected, inward, or stimulus-independent thought (Raichle et al. 2001). A more task-positive, attention-oriented network tends to trade off with it; the two can be anticorrelated even at rest (Fox, Snyder, Vincent, Corbetta, Van Essen, and Raichle 2005). That physiology is **supported**. Using it as a complete map of "Orun versus the kitchen" is **speculative**. The practical lesson does not need a scanner: inward and outward stances compete, and a cue can help you change stance.

**Planned daydreaming**, or scheduled absorption with an exit cue, is the design this evidence points toward. Set a time and a place to enter the inner world on purpose. Decide, in advance, what will end it — a timer, a bell, a doorway, a phrase, a living voice. That package is **plausible**. It borrows intentional mind-wandering, the value of a task cue (Rubinstein et al. 2001), implementation intentions (Gollwitzer 1999, already cited), and the idea that an event boundary can close one episode and open another (Section 5.4). It has not been tested as a named C-R-Z or Orun protocol.

Research on presence and absorption, including in virtual environments, shows that a well-built world can feel "here" (for a classic questionnaire approach, Witmer and Singer 1998). That is **supported** as a measurement tradition, and **plausible** as a description of why fiction is powerful. It does not, by itself, license any particular exit method, and it should not be over-read as proof that a person can inhabit two worlds at full strength at once.

### 5.2 The Drop: letting go of a complex or tempting thought

The author's Drop is already in C-R-Z as the first beat of Drop-Press-Snap, and in the Orun story as putting down the purse and the maps. This section is about using that same move on a thought that is complex, glittering, or hard to leave — and doing it as **letting go**, not as suppression.

Classic thought-stopping — a sudden "Stop!" meant to erase the thought — is a weak foundation. Wegner (1994) described ironic processes of mental control: an effortful attempt to push a thought away can be yoked to a monitor that keeps looking for that thought, and under load the unwanted thought becomes *more* available. That rebound risk is **supported**. It is one reason C-R-Z should not be taught as "crush the thought until it is gone."

What has a better neighbor in the clinic is not a fight. In Acceptance and Commitment Therapy, a person **defuses** from a thought by naming it as a thought — "I am having the thought that…" — rather than treating it as a command (Hayes, Strosahl, and Wilson 1999). The tug-of-war metaphor, often taught as **drop the rope**, makes the same point: you need not win against the thought; you can stop spending your strength on the struggle and step toward what matters. **Urge surfing** asks a person to feel a craving or a pull rise and fall, like a wave, without immediately acting on it (Bowen and Marlatt 2009, in a brief mindfulness intervention with smokers). These are **plausible** supports for a Drop that notices the thought, declines to wrestle it, and turns to a present cue. They are not a trial of C-R-Z, and defusion is a clinical method, not a parlor trick.

The form that ties this to the rest of the framework is an **implementation intention** plus a body drop. Gollwitzer (1999) showed that pre-decided if-then plans make a cue more likely to trigger a chosen act. The C-R-Z Drop, written that way, is:

> If I notice a thought that is looping, tempting, or pulling me out of this room, then I drop my shoulders, put my weight into my feet, let the thought be a thought, and look at one real thing (or one living face) here.

That is **plausible** as a package: meta-awareness supplies the "if," the body supplies a fast somatic interrupt, defusion supplies the stance (let go, do not suppress), and Reality-Return supplies the "then." It should be taught in those words, not as a guarantee that the thought will vanish.

### 5.3 Syncing back into a real conversation

Drifting in a conversation is ordinary. The repair is a skill, not a confession of failure.

Ordinary talk already has a system for fixing trouble. Schegloff, Jefferson, and Sacks (1977) described conversational repair, including a preference for people to correct themselves, and for others to prompt rather than overwrite. An honest sentence such as "Sorry, I drifted — can you say that last part again?" is **plausible** as good repair, and it is closer to that literature than bluffing or pretending you heard. There is no need to invent a new social science for the sentence. There is a need to give yourself permission to say it.

Once you are back, small **backchannel** cues (a nod, "mm," "I see") and a short paraphrase of what you just heard — reflective listening, in the tradition of Rogers and of later motivational interviewing — help the other person know you have landed, and help you check that you have. Those practices are **supported** as widely taught communication methods. As a snap-back kit they are **plausible**.

Re-anchor on what is actually in the conversation: the speaker's face, the sound of their voice, and their last words. A practical catch-up some listeners use is to replay the **last three words** they are sure they heard, and to ask from there. As a named method, that trick is **speculative**. As a use of the most recent speech still in ear and memory, it is **plausible**, and it is kinder than inventing the missing middle.

This is the story's own teaching in civic form. Relationships snap you back in reality. The living sentence, not the empty door, gets the reply.

### 5.4 Mental anchors for switching

Anchors are cues that help the mind change worlds on purpose, and find the way back.

**Singing or humming the thought.** Melody can make words more memorable. Wallace (1994) found that a melody could aid recall of text, under conditions that should not be over-generalized. Melodic intonation therapy has a clinical life in some aphasia work (Albert, Sparks, and Helm 1973), which is a different problem than everyday attention. Humming, because it lengthens the exhale, sits near the slow-breathing literature already graded in Section 3.6. Using a quiet hummed line, or singing a thought once in order to set it down and leave it, is **plausible** as a personal anchor. It is **speculative** as a special neural key, and it should be silent or nearly silent if a living conversation is in the room.

**The environment.** Outward naming (the 5-4-3-2-1 family, already discussed), a familiar object in the hand, a window, or the feel of a chair are present-tense cues. Context also colors memory: Godden and Baddeley (1975) found that divers recalled word lists better in the environment where they had learned them, land or water. That is **supported** for memory, and **plausible** as a reason to give the inner world and the real world slightly different physical cues — a chair for writing Orun, the kitchen table for the living meal — so that each place helps you return to its task.

**A short ritual to enter and leave the gate.** Walking through a doorway can make information from the previous room less available; Radvansky, Krawietz, and Tamplin (2011) treated doorways as event boundaries that update the current event model. That effect is **supported**. Using a small phrase, a gesture, or an actual threshold as a "gate" into and out of the fictional world is **plausible**. It is the same shape as the Orun tali: a gate that opens slowly, and that you can also close. The words can be ordinary ("I am going in"; "I am coming back"). They do not need to be a spell.

**Nature, bells, and a personal line.** Kaplan's attention restoration theory (1995) proposes that some natural settings can rest directed attention through "soft fascination." The theory is influential; the evidence is mixed in size and design, so the honest grade is **plausible** as a reason to step outside, not **supported** as a proven dose. A timer or a bell is a simple external cue, in the same family as mindfulness of the bell in contemplative practice. A verse or a short prayer can be a personal cue of the same kind. Those are **plausible** as signals, and **[Values]** as content.

### 5.5 A selu attention-budget model

Treat attention as a limited coin, like selu.

- There is only so much to spend in an hour. You cannot pay full price to Orun and full price to the person in the chair.
- **Real people have priority.** If a living voice, a child, a colleague, or a need in the room arises, the inner world yields. That is the commons rule, not a punishment of imagination.
- **Absorption is allowed**, on purpose, with a budget and an exit cue: a start, an intended span, and a way back (timer, doorway, phrase, body Drop, a name said in this hour).
- **Spending on reality restores** the account in the only sense that matters here: you return, you repair if you drifted, and the next visit to the inner world is leaveable again. This is akin to *Coherence Under Decay*'s rest that is not empty, and to residual decay that prevents a perfect hoarding shelter. An inner world with no nurev, no fade, and no exit becomes a vault.
- The sentence at the center is the author's: **Attention is life.** It is a values claim and a design rule. It is not a measured quantity, a currency, or a diagnosis.

The model is **speculative** as economics or neuroscience, and **plausible** as a compact way to remember the findings in Sections 5.1–5.4: switching has a cost, unnoticed drift has a cost, deliberate visits with a cue are safer than being quietly overwritten, and the living world gets first claim.

### 5.6 Take heart: the author's closing anchor

The author adds a conviction from his faith. In the Gospel of John, Jesus says, "In the world ye shall have tribulation: but be of good cheer; I have overcome the world" (John 16:33, King James Version). A common modern rendering is "Take heart; I have overcome the world."

This is presented as **the author's belief**, respectfully, and as a personal closing anchor: a higher power has already overcome this world, so a person may put a thought, a fear, or even a beloved inner world down, and come back. It is **[Values]**. It is not a clinical result, not a substitute for 988 or for care, and not evidence that C-R-Z works. A reader who does not share the faith can use the rest of the protocol without it. A reader who does may keep the verse as the last gate-phrase, the way other people might keep a breath, a name, or a bell.

## 6. The Connection Postulate: Purpose, Fantasy, and Presence

This section records a further postulate of the author's, offered in his own words on 13 November 2026, and then restated as seven numbered sub-postulates in plain English. The task, as in the rest of this note, is not to prove the postulate as a whole, and not to dilute it until it is only a slogan. It is to state it faithfully, set it beside the earlier architecture, and grade the parts that can meet a literature.

The author's wording, quoted so that the restatement can be checked against it:

> purpose is continually contemplated in the mind and only when techniques are used to connect to other people can the purposes end and accept fictional truths of actual techniques and mental ones fantasy is what connects us and also traps us the only way out of this story is for a need to honor people outside the mind and reality must continually be checked such as techniques to fight shame guilt and negativity tends to push false gossip similar to being caught in fantasy. there must be respect of God and other people who reach out to you to drop everything for genuine conversations and connection that triggers all senses. connection takes priority. based on this research find true ways to keep connections and sustain conversations memories and peace so when there are no more memories only love will remain the feeling of being loved in the end and surrender that all connections ended as they should be without seeking more knowledge when we can be grateful that energy and information will trigger the best point in time and the best knowledge to gain and create is being present to react than to seek.

### 6.1 The postulate, stated faithfully

The following seven sub-postulates are the author's. They are a clarifying restatement, not a replacement. Where a short title is added, it is only a handle.

- **P1. Purpose cycles in contemplation until connection completes it.** Purpose is continually contemplated in the mind. Techniques for connecting with other people are what can bring those purposes to an end, so that fictional and mental techniques can be accepted as techniques, rather than as the living aim.

- **P2. Fantasy both connects and traps.** Fantasy is what connects us, and also what traps us.

- **P3. The way out is honoring people outside the mind.** The only way out of this story is a need to honor people who exist outside the mind.

- **P4. Reality must be continually checked.** Shame, guilt, and a negativity bias tend to push false gossip and false inner narratives, which resemble being caught in fantasy. Techniques are needed to meet those drifts.

- **P5. Connection takes priority.** Respect for God, and for people who reach out, means dropping everything for a genuine conversation and a connection that can engage the senses. When a living person reaches out, connection comes first.

- **P6. When memories fade, love remains.** The hope is that, when there are no more memories, what remains is love: the felt sense of being loved, and a surrender that connections ended as they should, without a restless search for more knowledge, and with gratitude.

- **P7. Being present to respond beats seeking more knowledge.** The best knowledge to gain and create is being present to respond, rather than seeking on and on. Gratitude trusts that energy and information can meet the right moment, instead of trying to hoard every fact in advance.

P5's respect for God, P6's hope about love and surrender, and P7's trust that the right moment will arrive are **[Values]** as well as design rules. They are the author's convictions. They are not diminished by being labelled, and they are not turned into findings by being placed in a research note.

### 6.2 Cross-references

**Among the sub-postulates.** P1 through P3 form one arc: inner purpose keeps turning until a living person is honored, and fantasy is both the bridge and the snare. P4 is the maintenance rule for that arc: reality has to be checked, because shame, guilt, and negativity can invent a story that feels as closed as a daydream. P5 is the priority rule when a person actually reaches out. P6 and P7 are the long view: love as what is left when memory thins, presence as a better knowledge than endless seeking. They support one another if "the story" is the inner loop, and if "ending a purpose" means completing or setting down a mental errand by meeting someone real. They pull apart if "the only way out" or "only when" is read as a unique mechanism, or if "drop everything" is read as a duty to erase oneself.

**With C-R-Z.** Reality-Return is the nearest pillar: look at what is actually here, including the person in front of you. Calibration and the Drop (Sections 3.3 and 5.2) are the tools for interrupting a shame or gossip loop without wrestling it. Zero-Velocity is the rest that is not empty, which is close to P6's peace, and close to the warning in Section 4.2 that rest must not become isolation. The everyday architecture already treats a living voice as a valid key (Section 4.1). The tension to watch is sovereignty: the right to stop, refuse, and leave must not be rewritten as a closed system, and "drop everything" must not be rewritten as having no right to a boundary.

**With Attention as Selu.** P2 and P5 are almost the same claim as Section 5, said at the level of a life rather than a visit. Fantasy can be entered on purpose, with a budget and an exit cue; a living person can close the gate early; "Attention is life" is the author's sentence for that priority. P4's reality-check is kin to catching the drift and to syncing back (Sections 5.2–5.3). P1 adds something Section 5 did not quite say: that connecting with people is not only how one *returns*, but how a contemplated purpose can *complete*. That is a stronger claim, and it is graded as such in Section 6.3.

**With *Coherence Under Decay*.** The notes agree on bounded control, a rest that is not a void, clean egress, and the refusal to treat compulsion as virtue. Honoring people outside the mind is kin to the Circuit of Grace and to the warning that isolation is not sovereignty. P7 agrees with CUD if "do not seek on and on" means do not use compulsive checking or knowledge-hoarding as a substitute for a protected present. It conflicts with CUD, and with this note, if it is read as a ban on honest inquiry. CUD's standard should win here too: say what is a values rule, what is a design rule, and what would count as a measurement.

**A standing tension: P7 and the research enterprise.** This paper is itself an act of seeking. It gathers sources, grades claims, and proposes a pilot. P7 does not quietly cancel that work, and the work does not cancel P7. The honest reading, and the one used below, is narrower: seeking is not the highest knowledge when a living person is here, and it is not a peace when it is used to postpone presence, gratitude, or the end of a visit. A research note can still be written. It should not be used as a place to hide.

**With the Orun story.** "Drop everything, listen for a key to reality, and exit the gate," and the finding that relationships snap a person back, are the narrative form of P3 and P5. Fantasy as the culture's gift and its trap is the world of Orun itself. Songs, shared names, and the rule that one comes home only by choice are already a memory-and-consent practice. P6's hope that love remains when memory fades is a later sentence in the same key: a name, a feeling, and a return that does not have to be argued from a complete archive.

**P1, in more detail.** A century of work on unfinished tasks, often under the name of the Zeigarnik effect, found that incomplete aims tend to remain more available in memory than completed ones (Zeigarnik 1927). Martin and Tesser's goal-progress account of rumination says that thought keeps returning to a goal when progress is stalled, and that the loop eases when the goal is reached, when progress becomes manageable, or when the goal is relinquished (Martin and Tesser 1989, 1996). That is real support for "purpose is continually contemplated" until something completes or releases it. The author's distinctive clause is that *connecting with other people* is what can end the purpose, and that this is how fictional and mental techniques can be accepted as techniques. That is a reasonable design rule, and it fits the Orun finding that a living voice can snap a person back. It is not the only exit the rumination literature allows, and it has not been tested as a C-R-Z claim. The exclusivity of "only when" is therefore **speculative**.

**P2, in more detail.** Green and Brock (2000) described narrative transportation as a melding of attention, imagery, and feeling in which a person is absorbed into a story and may return somewhat changed, sometimes less able to notice the story's "false notes." Horton and Wohl (1956) described para-social interaction: a one-sided intimacy with a mediated figure that can feel like a relationship. Somer (2002), already cited in Section 5, described maladaptive daydreaming, in which vivid fantasy becomes hard to control and begins to interfere with life. Together these literatures **support** the two-sided claim: stories and inner worlds can connect people, teach them, and give them a place to stand, and they can also trap attention so that the living world is postponed. They do not decide the metaphysics of "this story." That remains the author's.

**P3, in more detail.** A meta-analysis of 148 prospective studies, with more than 300,000 participants, found that stronger social relationships were associated with a 50 percent greater likelihood of survival, an effect comparable to well-known risk factors for mortality (Holt-Lunstad, Smith, and Layton 2010, [open access](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000316)). The Harvard Study of Adult Development, a decades-long study of adult lives, has repeatedly found that the quality of close relationships is a central predictor of later happiness and health. Waldinger and Schulz (2010) reported, in a sample of married octogenarians from that tradition, that satisfying marriages buffered the daily-mood cost of health stressors; their later book-length account of the study puts the same lesson in public form (Waldinger and Schulz 2023). This **supports** the practical heart of P3: people outside the mind are not an optional decoration on a private purpose. It does not show that honoring them is the *only* way out of an inner story. That clause is **[Values]**.

**P4, in more detail.** "Bad is stronger than good" is the title, and the conclusion, of a wide review: negative events, feedback, and information tend to outweigh comparable positive ones in impact and in how thoroughly they are processed (Baumeister, Bratslavsky, Finkenauer, and Vohs 2001). That is strong support for a negativity bias that can tilt a story toward harm. Tangney and colleagues distinguish shame, which condemns the global self ("I am bad"), from guilt, which condemns a specific act ("I did something bad"); guilt more often leads toward repair, and shame more often toward hiding or defensiveness (Tangney, Miller, Flicker, and Barlow 1996; Tangney and Dearing 2002). Dunbar (2004) treats gossip as a human form of social grooming: a way of bonding, keeping track of a group, and sometimes policing it. That means gossip is not only a vice, and not only false. The author's narrower warning still stands as **plausible**: unchecked negativity, especially when mixed with shame, can generate and spread a story that has come loose from what can be checked, and living inside that story can feel like living inside a fantasy. Nearby tools include self-compassion (Neff 2003), compassion-focused therapy's work with shame (Gilbert 2009), cognitive defusion (Hayes, Strosahl, and Wilson 1999, already cited in Section 5), and dialectical behavior therapy's "check the facts" skill (Linehan 2015). These are **plausible** techniques for the drift P4 names. They are not a tested C-R-Z protocol, and they are not a reason to skip care when shame or rumination has become a clinical problem.

**P5, in more detail.** Partner "phubbing" — snubbing a person in favor of a phone — has been linked to more conflict over phone use and to lower relationship satisfaction (Roberts and David 2016). Even the mere presence of a mobile phone during a face-to-face conversation can reduce closeness and conversation quality, especially when the topic is personally meaningful (Przybylski and Weinstein 2013). Responsiveness research, taken up again in Section 6.4, finds that people feel closer when they are understood, validated, and cared for (Reis, Clark, and Holmes 2004), which is easier to do when attention is actually available. This **supports** the design rule that genuine conversation needs presence, and that a living reach-out should be able to interrupt an inner world, as Section 5 already said. It is **plausible**, not proven, that a conversation which can use face, voice, touch, and shared space is usually richer than text. "Triggers all senses" should be read as a wish for embodied presence, not as a requirement that every sense be engaged or that texted care does not count. Respect for God, and the rule that connection takes priority, remain **[Values]**. The bounds on "drop everything" are in Section 6.5.

**P6, in more detail.** This is the sub-postulate that must be handled with the most care, because it touches memory loss, dying, and grief. Feinstein, Duff, and Tranel (2010, [PNAS](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0914054107)) studied patients with severe amnesia after bilateral hippocampal damage. After sad or happy film clips, the patients quickly lost factual memory of what they had seen, yet their sadness or happiness persisted, sometimes more strongly than in comparison participants who still remembered the films. The honest lesson is sharp and limited: **a feeling can last after the memory of its cause is gone.** The study was small, it used laboratory inductions rather than a life of being loved, and it was a study of amnesia, not a dementia trial. It also ran in both directions. Sadness persisted too. So the finding **supports** "feelings can remain when memories do not." It does **not** support "only love will remain" as a prediction. That sentence is the author's hope and conviction, and it is labelled **[Values]**.

A second neighbor is socioemotional selectivity theory: as the time horizon shortens, people tend to prioritize emotionally meaningful relationships over information-seeking and weaker ties (Carstensen, Isaacowitz, and Charles 1999; see also Carstensen 2006). That **supports** a late-life turn toward love and presence, which is close to P6 and P7 as priorities. It is not a finding about what remains after memory is gone. Bronnie Ware's *The Top Five Regrets of the Dying* is sometimes cited in this neighborhood. It is a palliative-care nurse's anecdotal account, not a systematic study, and it is labelled as such here. Gratitude practices have experimental support for modest gains in well-being (Emmons and McCullough 2003), which can serve P6 as a practice, not as proof of its eschatology. "Surrender that all connections ended as they should" is **[Values]**. It must not be used to tell a grieving person to stop missing someone, or to treat complicated grief, abandonment, or harm as a tidy completion.

**P7, in more detail.** Brown and Ryan (2003) found that a disposition to mindful presence was associated with higher well-being. Dugas, Gosselin, and Ladouceur (2001) found that intolerance of uncertainty was specifically tied to worry, a pattern that often shows up as checking, rehearsing, and information-seeking that does not bring rest. Those findings make P7 **plausible** as a priority: when the alternative is anxious seeking, being present to the person and the moment in front of you is the better knowledge. The further claim, that energy and information will trigger the best point in time, is a trust statement, close to a theology of kairos, and is **[Values]** / **speculative** as a mechanism. The tension with this research note is real and should stay visible: the note may gather sources, and then it should be put down so that a living conversation can have the next unit of attention.

### 6.4 True ways to keep connections and sustain conversations, memories, and peace

The author asked this note to find, from the research, true ways to keep connections and to sustain conversations, memories, and peace, so that love can be what remains. What follows is a practical toolkit, not a treatment plan and not a promise. Each item is tagged by evidence tier, and each is mapped, where it helps, to a skill already named in C-R-Z, in Attention as Selu, or in the Orun story.

**Reaching-out rituals.** **Plausible** as a design, with **supported** neighbors. Do not wait for the perfect mood or the finished thought. Set a simple, repeatable contact — a weekly call, a shared meal, a standing walk, a short message that is actually meant — and keep it small enough to survive a hard week. Sharing good news, and receiving it well, adds something of its own: capitalization research finds that telling another person about a positive event, and being met with an active, constructive response, is linked to better daily affect and to closer relationships (Gable, Reis, Impett, and Asher 2004). In Orun terms, this is spending selu on the commons before the coin sits unused.

**Drop-everything rules for incoming connection, with healthy limits.** **[Values]** as a priority, **plausible** as a practice. When a person reaches out for a genuine conversation, pause the inner world, close the gate early, and arrive. That is already the priority rule in Section 5.7. The limit, stated gently and kept in view, is that "drop everything" is not a command to drop safety, consent, or one's own basic needs. A reach-out does not override a child who needs supervision, a task that cannot be abandoned without harm, a boundary already stated, or the difference between a living request and an endless stream of pings. Compulsive availability is not the same as honor. If the reach-out is a crisis, the honest move may be to stay and also to help the person get care, including **988** in the United States. Details are in Section 6.5.

**Conversation sustainers: open questions, responsiveness, and repair.** **Supported** as a family of findings; untested as a C-R-Z script. Perceived responsiveness — feeling understood, validated, and cared for — is a central path to closeness (Reis, Clark, and Holmes 2004). When someone shares a joy, an active-constructive reply (interest, a question, a face that actually lights up) tends to travel farther than a quiet nod or a turn to the downside (Gable et al. 2004). Open questions and a short paraphrase of what was just heard are ordinary ways to give that responsiveness. When attention has drifted, the repair in Section 5.3 still stands: "Sorry, I drifted. Can you say that last part again?" Then answer the living sentence. In Orun, this is the snap back that a living voice can give, and the grace of admitting one was gone.

**Shared memory practices: reminiscence, life-story work, shared rituals, and songs.** **Supported** for reminiscence and life-review as a class of interventions; **plausible** for everyday rituals and songs. A meta-analysis of 128 controlled studies found that reminiscence and life-review interventions can improve ego-integrity and reduce depression, with smaller effects on well-being, purpose, and social integration (Pinquart and Forstmeier 2012). That is clinical and structured work, often with older adults, and it is not a home recipe. The transferable practice is gentler: tell the stories together while they can still be told; keep the rituals that carry a name, a meal, a holiday, or a song; let music hold words that might otherwise be lost (the neighboring, limited literature on melody and recall is already cited in Section 5.4). In Orun, this is the song, the shared dance, and a name that can be carried when a fact begins to fade.

**Reality checks for shame and gossip.** **Plausible** as a bundle of existing skills. When a harsh story about the self or about someone else starts to close, pause and check it as a story. Name the feeling. Ask what, exactly, was done, and what is known, and what is being filled in. Distinguish shame ("I am bad") from guilt ("I did this, and I can repair it"). If the story is about another person, do not spread what you cannot stand behind. Defusion helps: "I am having the thought that…" is already in Section 5.2. DBT's "check the facts" is the same family (Linehan 2015). Self-compassion and, where needed, clinical work on shame are the kinder path, not thought-stopping (Neff 2003; Gilbert 2009; Wegner 1994, already cited). This is P4 in practice, and it is a Reality-Return for social narrative.

**Gratitude and surrender practices.** **Supported** for simple gratitude exercises as modest well-being practices; surrender is **[Values]**. Emmons and McCullough (2003) found that regularly noting blessings, compared with noting burdens, was associated with more positive affect in their experiments. A short end-of-visit or end-of-day thanks — for the person, for the time, for what was enough — is a low-friction form of that work. Surrender, in the author's sense, is not apathy and not a forced cheerfulness. It is the willingness to let a conversation, a purpose, or even a life-chapter end without immediately hunting for more knowledge to fill the space. If grief is complicated, persistent, or despairing, this practice is not enough; see Section 8.

**Legacy-of-love practices.** **Plausible** as an ethical design, with a limited **supported** neighbor in the feeling-after-memory finding. If feelings can outlast the facts of their cause, then how a person is treated now may still matter when the episode itself cannot be recalled, by them or by us. The honest version of that hope includes the warning from Feinstein, Duff, and Tranel (2010): unkindness can leave a feeling behind too. The practice is therefore ordinary and serious: be someone whose presence would be a good feeling to keep. Write the letter, keep the song, record the voice, say the name, and make repair while repair can be received. Then let the connection be complete enough to put down. In Orun, this is coming home by choice, and leaving a name that can still be loved.

### 6.5 Faith, values, and bounds

The author includes, as a conviction of faith, respect for God, and a hope that love remains when memory does not. As in Section 5.6, this is presented as **the author's belief**, respectfully, and as **[Values]**. It is not a clinical result, not a substitute for 988 or for care, and not evidence that C-R-Z or this postulate has been validated. A reader who does not share the faith can use the reality-check, the conversation tools, and the presence rule without it. A reader who does may keep the respect, the gratitude, and the earlier faith-anchor ("Take heart; I have overcome the world," John 16:33) as part of how they drop a story and meet a person.

**"Drop everything" needs bounds.** The priority is real: a genuine reach-out may close an inner gate early. The bound is also real. Connection does not require the abandonment of safety, of another person's consent, or of one's own basic needs. It does not require answering every request at once, remaining in a harmful conversation, or treating guilt as a proof that one has not given enough. Honor, in this note, includes the dignity of leaving, which the framework has already named as sovereignty. A person may say, "I want to give you my attention. I need a few minutes to finish this safely, and then I am here."

If you are in emotional crisis or thinking about suicide, in the United States, call or text **988**. If you are in immediate danger, call **911**. Nothing in this section is an instruction to delay emergency care, to leave prescribed treatment, or to use gratitude or surrender in place of help for depression, trauma, dementia care, or complicated grief.

<div class="protocol">
<p class="protocol-title">Protocol card: Connection takes priority</p>
<p>A short addendum to the Attention as selu card in Section 5.7, not a treatment.</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Notice the reach-out.</strong> A living person is asking for you, in the room, on the line, or at the door. That request can close an inner gate early. Connection takes priority.</li>
<li><strong>Drop, with bounds.</strong> Set the inner story down. Do not wrestle it. Check safety, consent, and whether you can actually arrive. If you need a brief, honest delay to finish something that cannot be dropped, say so, and then come.</li>

<li><strong>Sustain the conversation.</strong> Ask one open question. Reflect back a little of what you heard. If they share a joy, meet it actively. If you drifted, repair: "Sorry, I drifted. Can you say that last part again?"</li>
<li><strong>Check the story.</strong> If shame, guilt, or a harsh account of someone starts to take over, treat it as a story. Check the facts you actually have. Do not spread what you cannot stand behind. If repair is yours to make, make it.</li>
<li><strong>Close with thanks, and let it end.</strong> When the visit is done, mark the end. Be grateful for what was enough. Do not immediately hunt for more knowledge to fill the space. </ol>

## 7. A simple, testable pilot

C-R-Z has not been tested as a package. A first study should be small, pre-registered, and willing to fail.

**Design.** A within-subject laboratory or quiet-room comparison, in randomized order, of Drop-Press-Snap versus a control after a mild, ethically acceptable stress or worry induction (for example, a brief worry period or a standard mild cognitive stressor). This first pilot should not use trauma-script exposure. A third arm, if the sample allows, could be an established brief comparison such as a minute of 5-4-3-2-1 or of exhale-emphasized breathing, so that the question is not only "better than sitting still?" but "different from its nearest neighbor?"

**The C-R-Z arm, for the pilot, should use the reframed protocol:** drop the shoulders and put weight into the heels and soles (or into the feet if seated); press the big toe and the ground, described only as a tactile anchor, with no reflexology map; make the agreed motor cue (ASL "C" against the leg, or a simple agreed alternative) and name, quietly, two or three things in the room. The author's original wording can be taught as the personal form of the practice, but the *tested* instructions should not include unsupported organ claims.

**Control.** Sit or stand still for the same number of seconds, with no special cue, or count backward as a nonspecific cognitive control. The protocol's duration should be fixed in advance (for example, 30–60 seconds).

**Primary outcomes.** (1) Change in subjective distress, on a 0–100 visual analogue scale or a Subjective Units of Distress rating, from post-induction to post-technique. (2) Change in heart rate over the same window.

**Secondary outcomes.** Respiratory rate; if a device is already in place, heart-rate variability, clearly labelled secondary in light of Balban et al. (2023); a short state-anxiety scale; a one-item "how present do you feel?" rating; and, if Calibration is a target, a one-item rumination rating. Qualitative comments on whether the cue felt usable in the moment.

**Sample and ethics.** A feasibility sample (for example, a few dozen adults). Informed consent. Ethics or IRB review if the results will be published or if a clinical population is recruited. Exclude people in acute crisis, uncontrolled psychosis, or current manic or mixed episodes. People with bipolar disorder who are stable may take part only with a pre-specified plan and, where appropriate, clinician awareness; mania warning signs during the study are a stop rule, not more protocol. Offer a seated version for anyone with balance risk. Data private by default.

**What would support the design, as feasibility evidence only.** A pre-specified, modest reduction in distress after Drop-Press-Snap relative to control, with or without a short-lived heart-rate change, without serious adverse events, and with most participants able to complete the sequence. Effects should be reported with uncertainty, not as proof that C-R-Z "works."

**What would count against it.** No difference from control on distress; distress that worsens for more than a rare, transient case; heart-rate claims that were promised in advance and do not appear (in which case drop or demote the physiological story); participants finding the reflexology language confusing or untrustworthy; or any signal that people with rising manic signs used the protocol instead of seeking care.

This is a first measurement, in the same spirit as the small app pilot in *Coherence Under Decay*, Section 9. It is not a registration of a treatment.

## 8. Safety

C-R-Z is a personal practice framework. **It is not medical treatment, not a substitute for psychotherapy or medication, and not a crisis service.**

- In the United States, if you are in emotional crisis or thinking about suicide, call or text **988**. If you are in immediate danger, call **911**.
- If distress, panic, dissociation, or despair is severe, persistent, or worsening, contact a clinician or a local emergency service.
- **Mania or hypomania warning signs** — including markedly less need for sleep, a sustained burst of energy or confidence that others find unlike you, racing speech or spending, or a rising sense that ordinary limits no longer apply — warrant clinical follow-up. They are not "glimmers" to be ridden, and they are not a reason to intensify Drop-Press-Snap in place of care.
- People with trauma histories should keep the outward emphasis of Reality-Return. If a turn toward the body increases alarm or detachment, stop.
- The weight-shift in the Drop should not be done in a way that risks a fall. A seated version, with both feet on the floor, is a full version, not a lesser one.
- If immersive daydreaming, looping stories, or a fictional world begins to crowd out work, relationships, sleep, or ordinary care, that is a reason to seek professional help. Somer (2002) described **maladaptive daydreaming** as a pattern in which extensive, vivid fantasy becomes difficult to control and interferes with life. This protocol is not a treatment for that pattern.
- "Drop everything" for a genuine reach-out is a priority rule with bounds: it does not require abandoning safety, consent, or one's own basic needs, and it is not a duty of self-erasure.
- The hope that "only love will remain" is the author's conviction, not a clinical prediction. It is not a reason to delay dementia care, to skip support for caregivers, or to tell a grieving person that a connection has already ended as it should.
- Nothing in this note is an instruction to leave prescribed medication or to delay emergency care.

## 9. What this is not

- Not a validated treatment, diagnosis, or medical device.
- Not evidence that an ASL "C," a foot reflex point, or a triangle gaze has a unique neural pathway.
- Not a confirmation of polyvagal theory, of reflexology, or of power-posing physiology.
- Not a claim that economics, quantum mechanics, or fiction prove a somatic protocol. Cross-references in Section 4 are structural and narrative, not empirical transfer.
- Not a promise that threat can be neutralized on demand, or that sovereignty can be made absolute.
- Not a reason to ignore other people when a living voice is the key that works.
- Not a claim that true, cost-free multitasking is possible, or that a fictional world can be inhabited at full strength at the same time as a living conversation.
- Not a treatment for maladaptive daydreaming, and not a license to disappear into an inner world when life is asking for attention.
- Not clinical evidence for a verse of Scripture. John 16:33 is offered as the author's faith-anchor, not as a finding.
- Not a finding that only love remains after memory is gone. The nearest study shows that feelings, including sadness, can persist after memory of their cause is lost.
- Not a command to abandon learning, inquiry, or this research, and not a duty to drop safety, consent, or one's own needs in the name of connection.
- Not a treatment for complicated grief, clinical shame, social isolation, or dementia, and not a license to spread unverified gossip in the name of "checking reality."

## 10. Limitations

This audit is only as strong as the records checked on 13 November 2026, including the Attention as Selu sources and the Connection Postulate sources added the same day. The emotion-after-memory finding used in Section 6 is from a small study of hippocampal amnesia, not a dementia trial, and it does not single out love. Some nearby literatures (for example, sensorimotor psychotherapy and somatic experiencing) were not reviewed in depth and should not be treated as silently endorsed or silently dismissed. The three source papers were not available to this draft as public, dated, paginated documents; the framework is taken from the author's own synopsis, which he offered as the authoritative short form. If a later comparison with the full source papers shows a missed nuance, the source papers govern the description, and this audit should be revised.

The evidence tiers grade *component claims*, not the character of the author or the worth of the values layer. A speculative mechanism can sit next to a sound aim. The aim is not thereby proved, and the person is not thereby discredited.

A within-subject pilot will not settle long-term use, clinical populations, or the daily V2 adaptations. Demand characteristics are likely: people who believe in a sequence will often report that it helped. Pre-registration, a credible control, and at least one physiological measure that can fail are partial guards, not complete ones.

## 11. Recommendations

1. Keep the three-part shape: interrupt, look outward, come to rest on the ground.
2. Keep Drop-Press-Snap as a brief sequence, with the Press reframed as a toe-and-sole attention anchor, and with a documented alternative to the ASL "C."
3. Keep the breath, the open hands, and a soft outward gaze, described in ordinary language, without special-mechanism claims.
4. Replace "mania glimmers" with "mania early-warning signs" or "prodromes," and keep "glimmers," if at all, for small cues of present safety.
5. Write Zero-Velocity as an approach to rest (*v* approaching a floor, not *v* = 0), in line with *Coherence Under Decay*.
6. Let relationship stay inside Reality-Return: a living voice is a valid key.
7. Run the pilot in Section 7 before any public claim that the package regulates the vagus, hormones, or threat "at the hardware level."
8. Leave the values — sovereignty, grace, the right to leave, collective peace as an exhaled hope, "Attention is life," connection taking priority, and the hope that love remains — labelled as values.
9. Treat attention as a limited selu budget, with living people first, and allow inner-world absorption only with a span and an exit cue.
10. Teach the Drop as an if-then plus a body drop, and as letting go, not as thought-stopping.
11. Keep "Take heart; I have overcome the world" (John 16:33) in the values layer, as the author's closing anchor, not as a clinical claim.
12. Keep the Connection Postulate as the author's, with the seven sub-postulates graded in Section 6.3, and do not collapse "only when" or "only love will remain" into findings they have not earned.
13. Practice the drop-everything rule with bounds: safety, consent, and one's own needs remain standing.
14. Use the toolkit in Section 6.4 — reaching-out rituals, conversation sustainers, shared memory practices, reality checks for shame and gossip, gratitude, and a legacy of love — as ordinary practices, not as a treatment.
15. Do not let P7 cancel honest inquiry. Let it stop seeking that replaces presence, and let a visit end without hoarding more knowledge.
